# Session 29 — what the party will ask

Not an entity. DM prep for the questions five newcomers will throw at Ashford, gathered from the
data files on 2026-10-05. The `_` prefix keeps it out of `validate.ps1`, the semantic index, and
the session runner's plan pick.

Every answer is marked:

- **Say it** — in the data files and safe to say at the table.
- **Decide** — nothing in the files covers it. A suggested answer follows. Whatever you say
  becomes canon at `/recap`.
- **Hold** — true, but DM-only. A deflection line follows.

Townsfolk know their own valley and very little past it. "I don't know, I've never been" is a
canon-safe answer from anyone in Ashford, and it is the right answer more often than not.

## Settled 2026-10-05

Jeff answered these before Tuesday. They are safe to say at the table and get written into the

1. **The kingdom is Velmora.** Purple, the plains nation, is a **weak crown over powerful landed
   families**. Lord Cael's letters go to a court that is busy, broke, or doesn't care. This
   settles the clash between House Cael's "distant purple crown" and the Purple questionnaire's
   no-capital sketch.
2. **The border-river is the Moorthistle.** The tributary through Ashford stays unnamed ("the
   creek").
3. **The highland nation across the river is Riolgatha** (Red, the peninsula highlands). Locals
   use the name plainly.
4. **The sky has a broken moon.** One huge moon with a bite out of it, and a faint glittering
   ring of debris along its path. Give it one line in Scene 5, when someone steps outside.
   Locals don't look up. That indifference is what sells it.
5. **It is early autumn.** The harvest is in or coming in, so the stores the raiders hit are
   full. Locals say "after harvest," never a date.
6. **A few townsfolk saw the pillar of light,** mid-fight. By Scene 4 a rumour is going round:
   "they came down out of the sky." Mara asks about it once. The party chooses how to explain.
7. **Halsby is a well-read traveller.** Send Kellan the paragraph in the last section before
   Tuesday.
8. **Tassad is three weeks by road, or ten days by water.** Locals: *"Three weeks by road, or
   ten days if you catch a barge down the Moorthistle and a ship from the bay."* Measured on
   `maps/locations/calenmere-political.png`: about 900–1,250 km in a straight line, using the
   region's speeds (horse 60 km a day, barge 80 downstream, ship 150). The river is the real
   highway.
9. **Coin is plain gp, sp and cp,** with no special names.
10. **Healing comes from a roadside shrine to Lathander and a herb-wife** who sets bones. No
    priest in town can cast. There is also **a witch at the edge of things, Skyler's cameo**.
    She is a niche healer the village knows about and treats as risky to deal with. She doesn't
    harm the village. Nobody knows she is a vampire.
    - **She is the vampire Wick is hunting** (Wick's file: his guild detected a female vampire
      in the region, identity unknown). The party meets her first as a useful, risky healer.
      When Wick stages the bled body (act Session 3) and names her in public (act Session 4),
      the party already knows her and has a real choice to make. Tonight, locals mention her
      at most as "the one you go to when the herb-wife can't help, if you're desperate."
      Her name and where she lives wait on Skyler.
11. **Locals see the party as strangers, not freaks.** Sable has the town used to unusual
    blood. People stare because the party are outsiders who fell out of the sky, not because
    of race. The aasimar glow gets whispered about together with the pillar of light.
12. **Common works.** Humans and most of Aethryn's people speak Common, Velmorans and
    Riolgathans alike. A people speaks another language only where its file says so.
13. **The stolen delivery was worth 1,000 gp, and Sable had paid only a third.** She owes Silas
    about 650 gp. Silas's tangent lands on both numbers. She is close to ruined, and that's why
    the stake offer is desperate rather than generous. She'd never say so.
14. **Scene 5's room is Mara's people:** Mara, Silas, the wounded deputies and a few families.
    It is a smaller, quieter room than the plan's "full tavern."
    - **Plan impact:** Dip's gift (S1_IGGY step 1) wakes for "a gathering close to worship." A
      dozen grieving people singing together still counts. Play it as a vigil, not a party,
      and keep the roll as written.
15. **The raids have run two or three years.** It's a long, slow bleed. The town is worn down
    and cynical, several families have already left, and Lord Cael's letters look worse with
    every season. Tonight is the first strike to hit the whole town at once.
16. **Lord Cael's seat is a modest fortified hall 3–4 days' ride north.** He's too far off to
    come in time, but a letter or a visit is a real option later. The hall is unnamed.
17. **Maps are a layered handout.** Silas's road sketch is the base: roads, fords, and the
    Moorthistle down to the bay, with rough distances. Other people fill it in over time:
    - **Elowen** has a guild-drawn war map, decades out of date, which comes up in her scene.
    - **Halsby** carries a traveller's map of his route to Tassad.
    - **Mara** marks where the raids have hit. **Richmond** knows the old battle ground on the
      Moorthistle.
    - Each source adds a layer, so the map grows as the party earns standing in Ashford.

## Where are we?

- **"Where is this?"** — *Say it.* Ashford, a ford-town of a few hundred on a creek that runs
  down to the Moorthistle. It's named for the ford. The ford is how goods come in, and it's how
  the raiders come in too.
- **"What's the country?"** — *Say it.* Velmora. Ashford sits in Stonemarch, Lord Corren
  Cael's fief, on Velmora's southern frontier. Across the Moorthistle is Riolgatha.
- **"What's the world called?"** — *Say it,* with care. Aethryn is the world's name. A frontier
  sheriff would never say it. Halsby might.
- **"What's nearby?"** — *Say it.* Duskwater is southeast, along the river: greener country, and
  another house's land. Tassad is the big port on the bay, three weeks by road or ten days by
  water. Locals call it rich,
  crowded, and hungry. There are homesteads, the road, and not much else in between.
- **"Is there a map?"** — *Say it* (item 17). Silas's road sketch, with more layers from other
  townsfolk later. The town draft at `maps/battlemaps/ashford/` is DM-side and mostly not canon.

## Who's in charge?

- **"Who runs the town?"** — *Say it.* Silas Crubb is mayor. Mara Tull is sheriff, and most of
  the actual defence. Ashford keeps itself useful to everyone and loyal to no one out loud.
- **"Who's the lord?"** — *Say it.* Lord Corren Cael of House Cael. He's honest and he means
  well. He's stretched thin and he doesn't come. Mara: *"Lord Cael writes a very good letter.
  I've a drawer full."*
- **"Where is he?"** — *Say it.* At his hall, 3–4 days' ride north (item 16).
- **"Who's above him?"** — *Say it.* The Velmoran crown, which is weak, far off, and busy with
  its landed families. It doesn't send help.
- **"Is there a garrison?"** — *Say it.* No. Mara has deputies, and too few of them. There's an
  old mage who helps "when she's having a good day."

## The raid

- **"Who were they?"** — *Say it,* lightly. Bandits who call themselves independent. They hit
  the whole town at once, in separate crews.
- **"Who's behind it?"** — *Say it,* one rung only. Matched boots: *"Third crew this season that
  calls itself independent. Same cobbler."* Someone is paying them. Stop there.
- **"Who is Harrow Bane?"** — *Say it* as a silence. *"Bane's lot,"* and the talk stops. Mara:
  *"Not in the street."*
- **"Where did the cart go?"** — *Say it.* Southeast, a woman with a bow on the seat, and the
  ruts are deep.
- **House Marlow, the cutout, the old alliance** — *Hold.* Deflect: *"That's a long story and
  it isn't mine. Ask the smith, some day he's in the mood."*

- **The brews** — *Say it.* Lioness's Pride, The Standing, Pale Crossing, Embertide, Ironhold,

## Money and supplies

- **"What do people pay with?"** — *Say it.* Gold, silver and copper (item 9). Locals have
  heard of Whatwill pearls and never held one.
- **"Can we buy healing?"** — *Say it.* Healing potions at Silas's, marked up until the raids
  stop. No scrolls. No magic shop: the town is too small.
- **"Is there a temple, or a healer?"** — *Say it* (item 10). A roadside shrine to Lathander
  and a herb-wife. For worse cases there's the witch, and locals say so reluctantly. Real
  healing is magic, and magic is rare.
- **"Can we get our gear fixed?"** — *Say it.* Richmond the smith, though the raiders wrecked his
  stock.
- **"Where do we sleep?"** — *Say it.* The Lioness, free tonight, no strings.

## Magic, and how they look at us

- **"How do they react to our magic?"** — *Say it.* Magic is rare here, about three in ten on
  the table's own scale. In a small town it reads as awe, as something above, closer to legend
  than to neighbour. Five strangers who came down in a pillar of light and threw spells in the
  yard will be talked about for a year. Some will be frightened.
- **"Is it legal?"** — *Say it,* vaguely. The wizard guilds run like cartels. Some keep
  registries and police unlicensed casters, some don't. Nobody in Ashford enforces anything.
  Tassad is a different story.
- **"Do they stare at the aasimar?"** — *Say it* (item 11). The town is used to Sable. Luci
  and Iggy draw the looks strangers draw, and the glow gets folded into the sky-light rumour.
- **"Does our magic work right?"** — *Say it.* Yes. Gear from Barovia works normally. Only the
  things bound to Barovia's curse go quiet: the afflictions, and the Boon.

## Gods

- **"What gods do they pray to here?"** — *Say it.* There are two layers. The real devotion is to
  powers in the land itself. On the plains that's the old practice of the Grey Mothers, a
  practice more than a god. Over the top sit distant, formal "old gods" with temples in the
  cities: Waukeen for trade, Kelemvor for the dead, Lathander for the dawn and for oaths,
  Umberlee for the sea. Frontier folk name them at funerals and harvests and not much in between.
- **"Does my paladin oath / divine magic still work?"** — *Say it.* Yes. The pantheon is real
  enough for clerics and paladins.
- **Sah** — *Hold.* Iggy's beat in Scene 5 is the only touch, and nothing about it is narrated.

## The sky

- **"What's that in the sky?"** — *Say it.* The broken moon (item 4). Locals shrug: it's
  always been like that. Things a native would know, if you want them: an old body called Astra struck the world long ago, and broken islands still hang in
  the sky because of it. Glass falls in showers somewhere in the world most weeks, after a
  whistling sound called a Kerning. Rich men fight over where it lands. None of this has
  touched Ashford. It is news from other places.
- **The Whatwill economy** — *Hold* the politics. The act is the feud; the campaign's Whatwill
  conflict is still a pinned group discussion.

## Barovia, and getting home

- **"Has anyone heard of Barovia, or Strahd?"** — *Say it.* No. Blank looks.
- **"Can we go back?"** — *Say it,* through the fiction. The stone is dead, and that's the
  session's closing beat. Nobody in Ashford knows anything about it.
- **"Did anything follow us?"** — *Say it.* No. Nothing crossed with them, and nobody is chasing.
- **"What about Davian's gems?"** — *Say it.* One line, and nothing more. It isn't tonight's
  problem.

## Halsby — what a native knows

Halsby grew up in this world, in Sarmene, west near the shadow-wound. He is the party's only
local, and Kellan has been told only about the Nightveil errand. Suggested paragraph to send him:

> Halsby knows the world the way a well-read traveller does. He knows the sky: broken islands
> hang up there, glass falls in showers somewhere most weeks, and rich men fight over it. He
> knows magic is rare and the wizard guilds are cartels to be careful around. He knows the old
> gods by name. He knows Tassad by reputation: huge, rich, three towers over the harbour. He has
> never been to this valley, and he knows nothing of its houses or its feud.

- **Where Sarmene is relative to Ashford** — *Say it,* vaguely: "west, weeks away." Nothing on
  the map places it yet. That keeps Halsby a stranger here too.
- **The fallen flying city** — *Hold.* It's his backstory, and Kellan's to raise.
- **Bleth, Rivalen** — *Hold,* absolutely. House Bleth has a trading office in Tassad, and that's
  a later session.
