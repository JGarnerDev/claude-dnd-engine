Beneath **[[Ashford]]**, in the flooded caverns and the deep aquifer that feeds the
Moorthistle, lives **Aashal-Groon Mellenegon Borak**. He is one of a kind — no kin, no
species, nothing else like him anywhere. His true name is a pressure behind the eyes when
he gives it, which is why **[[Sable Wren]]** clipped it down to **Asher** in the first week
and never went back.

He reads as an aboleth crossed with a great red slaad (the red slaad's build, not its color): a vast frog-and-eel bulk, warty
blue-green-and-slate hide under a sheen of mucus, a broad amphibian maw, stacked slit eyes
that burn green and leak smoke,
trailing sensory tendrils. He is amphibious — the deep water is his, but he can haul himself
into open air. He does not speak aloud. He speaks mind to mind, in a voice too large for the
skull it arrives in.

Whether he is a god is a question nobody in Ashford has thought to ask. He is old, he is
singular, he answers a pact, and he withdraws his blessing from water that has been fouled.
That is close enough to divine for the purposes of anyone living above him.

## Mind and temperament

The two halves of him run at different speeds. The **aboleth** side carries immense
sediment-memory: he remembers the water when it ran clean, remembers every drowned thing in
it, knows what no living person knows, and takes his time about everything. The **slaad**
side is chaotic and moody, with a primal appetite and sudden turns — blackly funny, unbound
by any mortal manners.

The result is terrifying to meet and oddly good company afterward. He is dry, curious about
small mortal things, and warm in a lopsided way. Sable got past the fear; so can anyone
else, and that crossing is the whole trust beat with him.

## The grievance

He guards the purity of the deep water and the life it holds, from below. He tastes what
seeps down — blood at the fords, ash, the slow rot of the feud between
**[[House Cael]]** and **[[House Marlow]]**.

The sharp part is this: **the water is how he remembers.** Fouled water corrupts his memory,
so the feud is not merely poisoning his home, it is eating his mind. That gives him a
personal stake in a conflict he otherwise stands entirely outside of, and it is why he will
trade with surface agents at all.

There is a smaller insult underneath the large one. The river was never red. It ran clear
blue-green, his own color, lit from the deep. It runs red now only because of the feud's
blood, and the name **Redwater** is a scar the killing gave it. He remembers the river's
older, clean name, and does not give it away.

When he reaches up through the water to ride an animal, its eyes burn his green and leak
smoke. Anyone who has met him once knows the sign.

Where the water has turned, his blessing is already withdrawing: sick fish, bad water,
stretches that have gone wrong for no reason the townsfolk can name. It is discoverable
from the surface, and it points at the feud.

## Sable, and the pact

The cellar spring under **[[The Golden Lioness]]** drops into his aquifer. Sable was hired
to exorcise "the thing in the water." Instead she talked to it, was not disgusted by it, and
started bringing it the world's small news and good drink. In exchange she gets the cleanest
water in a fouling town, and the ability to bless and charge deep-water for potent brews.

The debt runs three ways, and none of it is evil:

1. Keep his water clean, and used for *life* — the Lioness as neutral watering-ground where
   feud-split townsfolk drink the same clean cup.
2. Oppose the fouling.
3. Keep him company and keep him informed. He is the last of himself.

## At the table

He is a reflavoured **Fathomless** patron — a unique being, not a published one — with
**Pact of the Tome** as the boon. Sable's tome is a rite-and-recipe ledger, part waterlogged
tablets hauled up from the cavern, part her own transcription of the fragments of deep memory
he mutters up the spring: histories, names, the river's forgotten clean-name. It grows as
the bargain does. His pact features all read the same way — *the deep thing lends its reach
through water*: a tendril sent up through any nearby pool, water-breathing and a swim speed,
travel by deep water.

Blessed deep-water is the gated input for enchanted brewing, which is how he plugs into the
base-building loop. He bargains for deeds that serve his water and his memory — cleanse a
fouled stretch, stop a killing at a ford, recover the drowned, or simply bring him a true
story. His chaos half is the risk on that reward: a bad-tempered turn fouls a batch, roils
the well, or sends a tendril somewhere it was not wanted. Mood, not malice.

His deep memory is a lore faucet with a tap on it. He may well know what the two houses are
actually arguing about. He does not give it away.

The joke underneath all of it: the safest building in town is sitting on a monster.

## Canon — Session 29

- **He reached out through a fox.** A possessed fox with glowing, smoky green eyes stopped
  [[Halsby]] on a night road and spoke into his mind. It asked him to save "one quite dear to
  me", [[Sable Wren]], from bandits at [[Ashford]] across the river. It said it could not
  intervene itself.
- **The fox's promise:** it sensed Halsby's spear and his "magical conduit, a stone or gem" and
  offered to join them into a boon, but **only after** the job: "You help me and I help you."
  Halsby took the job without haggling. The boon is [[The Shadowless Spear]], owed, and the DM
  plans to deliver it around Session 30–31.
- He was **never named** to Halsby, and the party does not know he exists. Only one animal (the
  fox) was used. No snake appeared.
