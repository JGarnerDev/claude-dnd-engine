## Your nations

### Red — Peninsula Highland realm

**Dots:** (8) Highland Seat, (28) Outer-Coast Port + (1), (2), (3), (5) — the southern spur and peninsula neck.

*Character — They hold the mountain ridge and one ocean-facing port, living off stone, ore, and wool from hard high country. Proud, guarded, comfortable defending high ground. Their problem: they believe the peninsula coast is theirs, and the coastal towns disagree.*

> **Largest coffee trader.** [[Coffee]] (yours) lands first at Outer-Coast Port (28) off the southwest ocean lane, so Red imports and re-ships more of it than anyone, even though the bay towns drink most. *(Draft left the factor-house port open — move it if you'd rather.)*
>
> *Miguel's seed:* the factor-house sits at Outer-Coast Port (28), and Red is proud of it the way a doorman is proud of a door he doesn't own. The Five Houses run the bean; Red runs the wharf the bean has to cross, and takes a landing-fee, a warehouse-fee, and a re-ship-fee at each stage. It reads as wealth from the outside. From the inside the Reachfolk know exactly what they are: the most expensive stretch of road on the coffee road, and roads don't set the price of what rolls over them. What House Tomsen's ledger thinks of Red's fees, and whether the factor-house is a guest or a garrison, is the DM's to settle with the Coffee canon — not mine.

**Starting stats** *(adjust freely):*

- Land size: Large — the whole mountain spine and peninsula, plus the southern spur.
- Population: ~4 million — humans ~70%, dwarves ~15% (the ore country), gnomes ~5%, rest mixed.
- Military: Strong on defense — high ground and passes; weak at projecting off the peninsula.
- Naval: Moderate — one real ocean port (28), for trade and refit, not war.
- Economy: Moderate-to-strong — stone, ore, wool, plus the coffee gateway.
- Ambition: Wants the peninsula coast and the strait forts under its rule.
- Problem: The coastal towns reject its claim.

**Name — the Ardvellyn Reach; its people, the Reachfolk.**

Named for the ridge, not the coast, which is the whole quarrel in a word. "Reach" means the high country — the long grasp of the Ardenwall down to the sea — and the Reachfolk say it to mean *everything the ridge overlooks*, coast included. The coastal towns hear the same word and understand it to stop where the rock stops. Ask a Reachfolk where the Reach ends and you get an answer about the watershed; ask a coast-town factor and you get an answer about the tide-line. Nobody has drawn the line because drawing it would decide the argument, and both sides would rather keep the argument than lose it.

**Government — the Highland Seat and the Ridge-Council.**

A crown that is really a chairmanship. The Seat at Highland Seat (8) is held by a single house, but it holds it at the pleasure of the Ridge-Council — the mining clans and wool-holdings who each control a pass, a vein, or a valley and send a voice to the Seat.

- **Who sits:** clan-heads of the ore country (dwarven, mostly) and the wool-lords of the high valleys (human, mostly), one voice per holding, weighted by what they can withhold. A clan that controls a pass the army needs speaks louder than its numbers.
- **What the Seat can do:** speak for the Reach outward — treat, war, set the coast-fees, garrison the passes. On the outside face, the crown is real.
- **What the Seat cannot do:** open a vein, move a clan, or override a holding on its own ground. The council granted the crown its outward voice and keeps everything inward for itself. A Seat that tried to tax a clan's ore directly would find the pass above the capital quietly closed.
- **Seat vs. Port:** the Seat sits in the mountains (8) by design — high, defensible, the old ground. The Port (28) is where the money is and everyone knows it, which is exactly why the Seat won't move there. To rule from the wharf would be to admit the coast is the country. So the crown stays on the rock and sends a governor down to the water, and the governor is always someone the Seat can afford to lose.

**One custom an outsider notices immediately — the disarming at the passes.**

You do not carry a drawn or ready weapon through a Reach pass. Peace-bind it, case it, or leave it — the pass-wardens will check, and a bared blade on the high road is read not as threat but as *insult*, a claim that you don't trust the ridge to keep its own road safe. The coast-towns, tellingly, don't observe this at all, and the Reachfolk cite that as proof the coast was never really theirs.

**Relation to the coast — a landlord who has never once collected in person.**

Red's claim to the peninsula coast is old, written, and completely untested below the tide-line. The coast-towns pay the landing-fees at the Port because the Port is Red's — that part is real and uncontested. What they reject is everything past the wharf: the levy, the muster, the right of the Seat to name their harbor-masters. For a hundred years neither side has forced it, because the Reach can't project force off the peninsula and the coast can't do without the Port. It's a claim kept alive by never being cashed.

*Unresolved:* whether the strait forts answer to the Seat, hold for the coast-towns, or quietly hold for themselves. Miguel's read is "for themselves, and both bigger powers pretend otherwise" — but that's a fault line worth the DM's hand, not mine.

*(Twist option left on the table: **Red as a dwarven realm proper** — flip the ratio, dwarves ~60%, the Ridge-Council becomes a clan-moot, the peninsula claim becomes an ancestral-stone claim, and the coffee gateway is run by the least caffeinated people imaginable. I've written it human-led with a strong dwarven ore-bloc, which keeps the twist a live door rather than closing it.)*

### Green — Storm Coast

**Dots:** (29) Storm Coast Harbor + unnumbered coastal dots along the outer northwest shore.

*Character — They hold the storm-battered outer coast — the only ocean shore that isn't Red's port, screened from everyone by the Ardenwall and reachable in practice only by sea. Winter closes the harbor for months; poverty and weather make them tough, insular, willing to raid when fishing fails. And they sit on two things worth killing for: the cliffs where [[Stormglass]] forms, and the one door into the region that pays no strait toll. Their problem: everything they have is worth exactly as much as their obscurity.*

**Starting stats** *(adjust freely):*

- Land size: Small — a long, thin strip of storm cliff coast.
- Population: ~200,000 — humans ~85%, half-orcs ~8%, a scatter of dwarves and drifters.
- Military: Weak — few numbers; the weather does the defending.
- Naval: Moderate for its size — the sea is the only road; storm-closed half the year.
- Economy: Weak on paper — fish, timber, off-the-books traffic through a toll-free shore, and an unmined secret (Stormglass). *The back door moves what's light and precious — coffee, glass, contraband, people, secrets — never grain or timber. It undercuts Cyan's dignity, not its revenue.*
- Ambition: Stay overlooked; keep the cliffs and the back door quietly their own.
- Problem: Poverty plus two hidden prizes — the moment anyone notices, isolation stops protecting them.

**Name — the Weatherlee; its people, lee-folk, or just "the coast" to themselves.**

They named themselves for shelter they don't actually have. *Lee* is the sheltered side, the calm water behind the land — and there is no lee on the storm coast, which is the joke, and they know it's the joke, and they keep the name anyway. Outsiders who don't get it think the Weatherlee must be a soft green harbor somewhere. That misunderstanding is the first line of defense: nobody sails looking for a hard place with a soft name.

**Government — captains and villages, and a Tide-Word when the weather forces one.**

Too poor and too scattered for a standing seat. Each cove is its own village under whoever owns the biggest boats; between them, nothing — until the sea makes a decision that no single cove can answer, and then the captains gather and speak a **Tide-Word**: a binding call for one season only. Raid or don't. Trade with this ship or sink it. Tell the outsiders this story about the cliffs. The Tide-Word dissolves when the season turns, and a captain who tried to make one last is put back in his boat.

- **Independent, or Red's province?** Nominally Red's — the Seat's maps color the storm coast as a far Reach holding, and Red has never once garrisoned it because the weather does the garrisoning for free. The lee-folk let Red keep the color on the map. It costs them nothing and it keeps Red from wondering why the coast isn't worth taking. *So: independent in every way that matters, a province in exactly the way that's cheapest to allow.*

**What they tell outsiders about the cliffs — that the cliffs are haunted, and they let you watch them believe it.**

The lee-folk story is that the storm cliffs are where the drowned go up instead of down — that the [[Stormglass]] is the drowned made brittle, and to quarry it is to be taken next storm. They tell it flatly, without selling it, the way you tell true things. It works because it's half-true from the inside: people *do* die on the cliffs harvesting glass in the storms it takes to form it, and the lee-folk have simply declined to correct the outsider's conclusion about *why*.

*Unresolved:* whether the haunting is only a good lie, or whether generations of dying on the same cliffs for the same glass have made it, by now, a little bit true. DM's cliff.

*(Twist option left on the table: **Green as storm-blooded** — goliath stock or storm-touched genasi in the oldest cove families, giving the Stormglass a "the cliffs chose us" dimension. I've written the ~8% half-orc line and the flat, weathered temperament so this twist can be switched on later without rewriting anyone — the storm-blood would just be the *deepest* families, not the whole coast.)*

## Your region-wide wires (Part 2)

*Not any one nation's — the live wires on your sea lane.*

### The Myriad — the island waypoints on the southwest lane

*(The Myriad is [[The Myriad|Ben's]] entity — waypoints, easy passage, "nobody lives there by preference." I'm not claiming or reinterpreting it; what follows is only what **Red and Green believe** about it, which is my ground to write.)*

The coast powers treat the empty islands as a standoff nobody signed. From Red's Seat and Green's coves alike, the Myriad reads as a place left uninhabited *on purpose* — every power on the bay would rather the lane stay one thin thread than watch a rival plant a fort halfway down it, so the first to garrison it starts the fight. Whether that's actually why the islands are empty, or whether they're just rocks and anchorages the way Ben wrote them, neither coast has tested — the belief does the work regardless.

*Left for Ben / the DM, not decided here: whether anything is really out there. If the Myriad ever wants a landlord — sea-folk enforcing a claim from below, a triton or merfolk reading — that's Ben's island to grant, and it would turn the coast powers' "standoff" into a courtesy they don't know they're paying. Flagging it as a live door, not walking through it.*

### Coffee factor-house — confirmed at Outer-Coast Port (28), in Red

The [[Coffee]] monopoly runs its landing factor-house at (28) — see Red's seed above. First ocean landfall off the southwest lane, on the one real ocean port in the region, sitting under a realm that takes fees but doesn't set prices. If the DM would rather the factor-house sit somewhere with less of a landlord, moving it off Red weakens Red's whole economic spine, so I'd keep it here and let the tension be the point.

### The southwest sea lane — the ship that came in unhailed

A hook to plant, not solve: a coffee-hull made the Port (28) off the lane last autumn riding low and correct — House colors, House seals, a clean manifest — and answered no hail on the approach. It docked itself. The factor-house checked the seals, checked the ledger-grade, found everything in order, and found no living soul aboard and no sign of struggle, only a hold of sealed crates and one crate short of the manifest.

*Unresolved:* whether the missing crate was fertile seed (and someone got off the Myriad with it), a Weatherlee back-door job that went too clean, or nothing to do with coffee at all and the ship is the lane's older problem wearing House colors. Three readings, all live, all cheap to trip.

## Hooks & NPCs *(optional — for your nations)*

If any spark, jot a rough hook or NPC seed tied to Red, Green, or the lane — one or two lines, don't solve them, just plant. Archetypes fine (a power-holder, a broker, a cliff-scavenger who knows the Stormglass, a foreign captain off the lane). The group grows them out.

**The governor the Seat can afford to lose.** Red's governor at the Port (28) — sent down from Highland Seat (8) to run the coffee wharf, and chosen, as always, from a house the Seat wouldn't grieve. He has been down at the water long enough to understand that the money, the ships, and the actual future of the Reach are all *here*, not on the rock — and to notice that the Seat picked him precisely because he was disposable. Unresolved: whether he stays loyal to the mountain that threw him away, or becomes the first Reachfolk to say out loud that the coast is the country.

**The cliff-scavenger who counts.** A Weatherlee woman who works the storm cliffs for [[Stormglass]] and has quietly kept a tally her whole life — how much forms, how much the sea takes, how much leaves through the back door and to whom. She believes the haunting story less than anyone alive and is therefore the most valuable and most dangerous person on the coast: the only one who knows what the cliffs are actually worth. Unresolved: whether she's kept the tally to protect the coast or to sell it.

**The captain off the lane.** A foreign shipmaster who runs the southwest lane past the Myriad more often than trade explains, hails Red's Port correctly every time, and once — only once, in a blow — put in at a Weatherlee cove he had no business knowing. He's known, liked, and useful to both coasts, which is exactly why nobody on either has asked the obvious question. Unresolved: who he actually carries for, and which of the two coasts finds out first.
