New to the table? A quick look

One party, two worlds, rotating DMs

We're five friends playing D&D 5e online over Roll20 and voice chat, 30-odd sessions in. The same characters bounce between two very different campaigns, and we keep the whole thing in a Git repo that an AI assistant helps us run.

How we play

  • Seasons, not one long campaign. We alternate between two worlds like TV seasons. The party carries over, crossing between them through a deliberate story beat.
  • The DM chair moves. Kellan runs Curse of Strahd. The homebrew chair rotates: Jeff ran the first season, Ben takes the next one. Whoever isn't DMing plays.
  • Everyone builds the world. All of us pitch places, people, factions and rumors into a shared pool. The DM pulls from it, so the world has everyone's fingerprints on it.
  • Choices matter. Every session plans in a few real decisions for the party, and the world remembers what we picked.
  • A bit of a game show on top. After each session there's a recap deck with silly achievements, stat awards (killing blows, nat 1s) and fame/infamy scores for the characters.

Running now · Homebrew

Redwater, in Aethryn

DM: Jeff · our own world

A world under a broken moon, where glass shards called Whatwill still rain down. Every faction wants to reach it first. Big-scale politics and intrigue, but warm, with rare magic. Ben runs the next Aethryn story after this one.

The Whatwill is pearly, glowing and unstable, and nobody can predict where the next Showering lands. A religious order reveres it, scholars cut it open, thieves' guilds steal it, the courts deadlock over who owns the ground it fell on, and whole towns float on it. Magic is rare and tells you who's on top. The party walked in as strangers with no side, which makes them very useful to everyone and easy to throw away.

Crossing between worlds

The crossing happens in the story, not offscreen. Near the end of the Strahd season the party got hold of a relic from a wizard at a Barovian winery and used it to step out of the mists into Aethryn. They brought baggage with them: a debt still owed to a family back in Barovia, and curses that went quiet once they left. One character carries a voice that whispers, another a shadow. Neither does anything in Aethryn, and both will wake up when we go back. Coming back to Barovia will be its own story beat too, and whatever happened in Aethryn comes along.

Sharing the DM chair

Rotating means nobody burns out behind the screen, and everyone gets a turn to play. When a world changes hands, the new DM's character steps out of the story for that run, written in as a trip of their own rather than vanishing. The outgoing DM decides which secrets pass on and which stay buried, then writes the incoming DM a one-page brief: where things stand, what's still loose, and what's now theirs to change. The new DM can take the world somewhere the last one never planned.

How the repo works

The campaign lives in a folder of plain text notes, tracked in Git. Claude (an AI assistant) works inside it with us: it reads the notes, keeps them straight, and drafts things for the DM to edit. It never decides the story. The DM does.

  1. PlayWe play on Roll20 and record the session.
  2. RecapThe recording gets transcribed, and the DM confirms what actually happened.
  3. RememberThat becomes the official history: who we met, what we broke, who likes us now.
  4. PlanThe next session is drafted from open threads and the idea pool, then the DM reworks it.

One session, start to finish

After a session the recording goes through local speech-to-text, and Claude turns the transcript into rough notes, marking anything it isn't sure of. The DM then walks through a recap with it, one question at a time: did this happen, is this NPC new, how does this character feel about the party now? Only what the DM confirms goes into the history. When it's time to plan, Claude reads that history, finds threads we've dropped, checks whose character hasn't had a moment lately, and drafts a skeleton of the next session. The DM rewrites it, cuts what doesn't fit, and runs it. Players add to the idea pool through short questionnaires about a place or person they want in the world. Nobody needs to know any of this to play.

For Strahd it only does the remembering, since Kellan preps that one outside it. For the homebrew it does the whole loop.

What we want from the game

We keep a wishlist the DMs draw from: an Ocean's 11 heist where the plan falls apart, a Kill Bill revenge list, court cases where evidence is the weapon, a catastrophe that hits on a festival day, a tragic villain with a god complex. And, someday, actual gorillas using guerrilla tactics. Combat, roleplay and mystery all get their turn.

Questions? Ask any of us. Happy to show you a recap deck or a session plan.
