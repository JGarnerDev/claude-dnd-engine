# The calendar breaks its promise

Saved from `data/lore/calendar-proposal.md` when it was retired at the calendar ingest
(2026-11-08). Its vocabulary and sky bodies were overruled; these ideas were not. Rewritten into the
canon terms (Showering, Crawl, Glistening, Fida).

## The core idea

Every rhythm in [[World Calendar]] is trusted by the world and can fail in play: the Showerings, the
Crawls, the thousand-year Glistening. Pick one and break it during the campaign.

1. **The gap grows.** Showerings "never" come more than ten days apart. Then twelve, then fifteen.
   The Kernings stop. Some towns celebrate; others read it as Astra abandoning them.
2. **The Crawl that won't end.** No major landing for sixty days. Trade on landing-site futures
   seizes up, and every Partenariat buyer is in the same town at once.
3. **The early Glistening.** Ties to [[The Calendarist's Early Glistening]]: the suppressed date
   turns out to be right, and Astra starts to brighten 257 years early.

## Three lenses on one calendar

Regional flavour: the same calendar, lived three ways.

1. **Strike-reckoning** (a battered frontier). Children count days since the last fall before they
   learn anything else. Roofs are steep and doubled. Markets close early. Weddings land in the fat
   middle of a Showering gap. Burials wait one gap in case the ground reshapes.
2. **The almanac** (a farming heartland). People ask about Fida and the length of tonight's dark,
   not the date. Hand-copied almanacs are heirlooms; a village sky-reader is valued like a healer.
   Strikes are weather.
3. **The sky that lies** (a devout or remote culture). Nobody experiences the count as numbers, only
   signs. Birth under a major landing marks a child, blessed or doomed by local creed. A broken
   promise from the sky is theology, not error.
