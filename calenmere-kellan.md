## Your nation

### Orange — Far-east forest interior

**Dots:** unnumbered dots deep in the eastern forest, past the league.

*Character — They live deep in Forest A's interior, past Yellow's towns, screened by trees in every direction. Everything they sell — timber, furs, whatever the deep woods yield — reaches market through the league's posts, so Yellow speaks for them to the world whether they like it or not. Their problem: the league stands between them and every buyer, and the unmapped deep forest stands at their back.*

> **Whatwill hinterland.** The debris field near Forest Edge (24) sits on their doorstep — if [[Whatwill]] salvage still comes out of the deep woods, it comes through or from Orange ground. *(Coordinate with Paul, who owns the Whatwill seam.)*
>
> *Kellan's seed:* the forest-side salvage moves as an ordinary forest good, unremarked, mixed into fur and timber loads at the posts — the Thelven do not treat it as special and have no theology about it, which means Paul's factions have been buying pearl-grade glass at fur prices from people who don't know what it is, or from people perfectly content to let them think so. Whether the court knows, and how the deep-wood courtesy relates to a field of fallen sky, is Paul's and the DM's to settle — not mine.

**Starting stats** *(adjust freely):*

- Land size: Moderate — deep-forest interior, borders as soft as treelines.
- Population: ~400,000, maybe — nobody has counted. Wood elves ~40%, humans ~35%, firbolg and other forest-kin ~15%, rest unknown. The only nation where humans aren't the majority.
- Military: Weak in the open, dangerous at home — scattered, forest-screened, unfindable.
- Naval: None — landlocked woodland.
- Economy: Weak — forest goods with no market access Yellow doesn't control.
- Ambition: Be left alone; deal with the deep forest on its own terms.
- Problem: Dependent on Yellow's posts to sell anything; whatever's deeper in Forest A is its neighbor first.

**Name — the Thelvenwood; its people, the Thelven.**

The word is a people first and a territory second, which is the whole problem for anyone drawing borders. Ask a Thelven where the Thelvenwood ends and you get an answer about who lives there, not where the line runs. Yellow's clerks write "Thelvenwood" on the tally sheets and mean the orange blot; the Thelven mean everyone who answers a summons to the court. Those are not the same set, and the difference is roughly a hundred thousand people nobody has agreed how to count.

**Government — the Thelven wood-court.**

A real seat with real authority, deliberately hard to reach. It sits in the interior at a place the Thelven don't name to outsiders — the tracks to it are unmarked and the last two days of the approach are through country that requires a guide who has to *agree* to guide you.

- **Who sits:** speakers sent by the kin-bands, one per band, weighted by nothing — a band of four hundred and a band of nine thousand each get a voice. Firbolg holdings send speakers too. The human settlements inside the wood send them as well, and have for long enough that nobody treats it as a concession anymore.
- **How it convenes:** continuously, in the sense that it is never dissolved; and rarely, in the sense that a full sitting takes a season to gather. Small matters are handled by whoever is present. Large ones wait, and the waiting is understood as part of the deliberation rather than a failure of it.
- **What it can bind:** anything touching the outside. Trade terms, treaties, whether a road gets cut, whether an outsider walks in. On these the court speaks for the whole wood and the bands hold to it.
- **What it cannot:** anything internal. It cannot tax a band, move one, settle a feud between two, or tell any of them how to live. A Thelven speaker who came home with an internal ruling would be laughed at.

The practical shape of that: the Thelvenwood can sign a treaty and *keep* it, which makes it far more dangerous to the bay powers than a scatter of bands would be — and it can be bargained with only on a timescale no merchant league has patience for.

**Relation to Yellow — it predates the league, and does not consider this an interesting fact.**

The Thelven were holding court under the same canopy before the first post at the treeline was a stockade. From the inside, Yellow's league is a recent encrustation on the forest's edge — a thing that grew up in a couple of centuries and will presumably come off in a couple more, the way such things do. They sell through the posts because the posts happen to be there. There is no grievance in it, which is exactly what unsettles the factors: you cannot buy off contempt with a better rate.

The gap that makes this playable:

- **Yellow believes** it is dealing with a backward supplier that depends on it for market access, and prices accordingly. The margin the league takes is understood, on the league's side, as the price of civilization.
- **The Thelven believe** they are humoring a tenant. They tolerate the posts the way you tolerate weather.
- **Neither has tested it.** Nobody in the league has asked what happens if the wood stops selling for one season; nobody in the court has asked what the bands would actually do if the court told them to.

**What comes out of the forest behind them** *(rough seed — the deep interior is the DM's)*

The court's outward-facing authority exists because something inward-facing made it necessary. The Thelven talk about the deep wood the way people talk about a landlord.

- Bands nearest the interior send speakers most reliably and say least about why.
- There are places the guides won't go, and the refusals are old enough to be etiquette rather than fear.
- Something out there is owed a courtesy, and the Thelven pay it without discussing it in front of outsiders.

That's the seed. What's actually back there, whether the courtesy is a bargain or a tribute or a habit whose reason has been forgotten — DM's call.

## Your region-wide wire (Part 2) — Non-human polities

*You take the deep-forest / unfindable peoples; Jeff takes the settled ones (dwarf clans, plains folk).*

### Do the forest-kin have their own nations inside these borders, or live under others' rule?

Neither, and the mismatch is the answer. The basin's political map is drawn in **territory**; the forest-kin organize in **membership**. A wood elf band at Yellow's margin (18/24) is inside league jurisdiction on any chart and inside the Thelvenwood by its own reckoning, and sees no contradiction — it pays the league's fees the way it pays for anything, and sends a speaker east when the court gathers.

So the margin forest-kin are dual-belonging rather than subject. Practical consequences:

- Yellow's rule over its forest-kin subjects is real for taxation and unreal for loyalty, and the league has never had cause to discover the difference.
- The Thelvenwood's actual reach extends past the orange blot into Yellow's territory, invisibly. Nobody has mapped it because the mapping convention can't hold it.
- Any bay power that forces a choice — a loyalty oath, a conscription, a border closure — converts a hundred years of comfortable ambiguity into a defection overnight. That's the region's quiet fault line and it's cheap to trip.

### Is Orange a polity, a scatter of kin-bands, or something that doesn't map to "nation" at all?

A polity, genuinely — but a **membership polity**, not a territorial one. It can deliberate, bind itself, and keep an agreement. It cannot be invaded in any meaningful sense, because there is no capital to take and taking ground doesn't take people. It also can't defend a line, project force, or do anything on a merchant's timescale.

Treat it as a nation with the axes rotated: strong where states are weak, weak where states are strong.

### How does the deep forest see the bay powers — trading partners, threats, or beneath notice?

Three depths, and they disagree, which is what the court exists to reconcile:

- **The margin** (bands trading at the posts) sees trading partners, and increasingly sees a bad deal — these are the ones with a grievance, because they're the only ones who know what things sell for on the far side.
- **The interior** sees weather. Bay politics are a thing that happens downhill and washes out before it arrives.
- **The deepest bands** don't see the bay powers at all. They see whatever is behind them, and consider the entire argument about ports and tolls to be the shallow end of a much older problem.

The court's job, though nobody phrases it this way, is keeping those three from concluding different things loudly enough to matter.

## Hooks & NPCs *(optional — for your nation)*

If any spark, jot a rough hook or NPC seed tied to Orange or the deep forest — one or two lines, don't solve them. Archetypes fine (a wood-court speaker, a firbolg who trades at the league posts, a forester screwed by Yellow's monopoly). The group grows them out.

**The speaker who came out.** A wood-court speaker from one of the deepest bands, currently at the treeline — the first from her band to leave the interior in living memory. She is asking specific, unfriendly questions about what the posts pay, and nobody can work out whether the court sent her or she came on her own. Unresolved: which.

**The firbolg factor.** A firbolg who has traded at the league posts for sixty years and is the only person the factors deal with regularly enough to consider a friend. He is comfortable at the treeline, well-liked, and has never once said which band he speaks for. Yellow assumes he's a broker. Nobody has checked.

**The forester with the second buyer.** A margin forester, human, born inside the wood and taxed by the league, who has found someone else willing to buy — and hasn't told the court, because telling the court means the court decides. Unresolved: who the buyer is, and whether the margin follows him if it comes out.
