Location: [[Stonemarch]]

Race: [[Human]]

Head of **[[House Cael]]** and lord of **[[Stonemarch]]**, the purple frontier fief that
contains **[[Ashford]]**. He is the town's lord and means well, which in practice is most of
the problem: he is bound to a distant purple crown, under-resourced, nominally responsible
for ground he cannot cover, and watching his people get robbed at a pace he can neither
match nor prove.

He rules from a modest fortified hall 3–4 days' ride north of Ashford. That is too far to
answer a raid in time, and near enough that a letter, or a visit, is always a real option. The
crown he is bound to is **Velmora**'s: weak, far off, and busy with its landed families.

## The grave-debt

Two generations back, **[[House Marlow]]** marched to House Cael's defense, and Corren's
grandfather died holding the Moorthistle beside Marlow's lord — shielding him, specifically.
A life owed, not merely an alliance honoured. That debt is now pointed the wrong way round:
the house his grandfather died for is the house bleeding his.

This is the hinge of the man. He cannot hate Marlow cleanly, and so he keeps answering raids
with protests and letters instead of with soldiers he does not have anyway. It is
simultaneously his weakness and the only genuinely dignified thing about his position.
Ashford reads it, charitably, as a lord who is trying. Less charitably, as a lord who will
not come.

## At the table

He is the legitimate authority the party can appeal to, and the appeal will not be enough —
which is precisely why the base-building offer at **[[The Golden Lioness]]** lands. He is not
a villain and not a fool. He is the correct answer that does not work.

If the party ever hands him proof that Marlow ordered the raids, the question stops being
whether he believes it and becomes whether he can act on it. Neither of those is a given.
