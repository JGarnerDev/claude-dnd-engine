# Calendar & Astronomy — Draft Proposal

*Draft for group review. Built from earlier world-history notes plus the Whatwill/Astra canon (`data/resources/Whatwill.md`, `data/locations/regions/Astra.md`). Not yet canon — once an option is picked, this ingests into `data/lore/calendar.md` and `data/lore/history.md`.*

*The unresolved decisions this proposal needs are question files under `questionnaires/world/history/calendar-proposal/`.*

---

## Locked Decisions

These were confirmed before drafting and hold across all three options:

- **Year 0 = the [[Astra]] Impact.** The collision that created [[Whatwill]] is the founding event. Present day ≈ **year 6743** (canon dates the impact at 6.7433 millennia ago). Deep-time feel, big numbers.
- **Vocabulary follows the new notes**, not the older Whatwill/Astra files:
  - **Keening** — the monthly major strike that devastates and transforms a stretch of land.
  - **Showerings** — the rare (every 1–2 decades) massive strikes that lift land into the sky as Islifts/Slices.
  - **Glistening** — the millennial return of Astra.
  - *(Canon-update flag: `Whatwill.md` and `Astra.md` currently use "Showering" for the weekly strike and "Kerning/Crawl/Opticon" for other tiers. Those files need reconciling to this vocabulary when the proposal is canonized — see `questionnaires/world/history/calendar-proposal/canon-reconciliation.md`.)*
- **Sky bodies are light flavor, not structural.** The calendar count stays Earth-like (7-day week, 12 months, ~360 days). The two suns / three moons / nine planets color daily life, festivals, and omens — they do not redefine the underlying math.

## Shared Structure (all options)

- **Day** — Earth-standard.
- **Week** — fixed **7 days**, Earth-like. Sample weekday names (Roman-elven hybrid, English-adjacent): Prima, Duosae, Tervex, Quartis, **Nortes**, Senael, Solrest. (The "nort"-sounding day requested in the notes = **Nortes**.)
- **Month** — 12 per year. Draft names (Roman-elven, English-adjacent): Janvar, Fevral, Maris, Avrel, Mael, Junae, Quintar, Sextil, Velnar, Octen, Novren, Decath. *(Names are placeholders — finalize during ingestion.)*
- **Year** — ~360 days, numbered from the Astra Impact (the First Fall / First Gleaming, depending on option).
- **The strike cadence is an overlay**, not the civil week. Debris falls roughly weekly (every 4–10 days) but drifts against the fixed 7-day week — that misalignment is the source of everyday tension in all three options.

### The Cosmic Ladder (shared cadence of phenomena)

From fastest to rarest. **Every periodicity below is assumed reliable by the world but is secretly fallible** — and is meant to be proven shockingly wrong during the campaign:

- **Weekly (4–10 days)** — minor debris strike, heralded by a herald-whistle.
- **Monthly** — a **Keening**: a major strike devastates and transforms a stretch of land (creates a Lensing Zone — glowing soil, alien/carnivorous growth, phantasms).
- **Every 1–2 decades** — a **Showering**: a massive strike lifts a chunk of land into the sky (new Islift / Slice).
- **Mid-millennium** — a global **dampening or surge of magic**.
- **Millennium** — a **Glistening**: Astra returns on its elliptical orbit.

### Sky Bodies (shared flavor)

- **Two suns** — **Aurel** (bright gold primary) and **Veska** (dim pale companion). Both up = *full-light* (no true shadow, fast growth, hot tempers); Veska alone = *lesser-day*, a long bronze twilight.
- **Three moons** — **Maru, Lethe, Ossin**, each on its own cycle.
- **Nine planets** — distant wanderers, mostly omen-fodder; not yet detailed.

## The Three Options

The skeleton is identical; the options differ in **what the calendar means to ordinary people** and which layer of life it organizes.

### Option 1 — The Strike-Reckoning *(life lived between falls)*

Time is organized around the bombardment. Year-0 event: **the First Fall** (the impact that began all later falls); present = *Anno 6743 of the First Fall*.

Everyday texture:

- Children count "days-since-fall" before they learn month names.
- Roofs are steep and double-layered ("strike-pitched"); a flat roof marks a fool or a foreigner.
- Markets open at dawn and close early — nobody wants to be in the open square when a herald-whistle sounds.
- Weddings and harvests are scheduled in the *fat middle* of a strike-gap, never near day 4 or day 9.
- Mourning waits one full strike-cycle before burial — the ground might still reshape.
- The monthly **Keening** is a civic event: a "fall-warden" reads the sky and rings the deep bell; afterward the town sees *where* it landed, and local economies pivot overnight on the new Lensing Zone.

Fallibility hook: the strike-gap, which "never exceeds ten days," starts skipping — twelve days, then fifteen. The bells fall silent. Some towns celebrate deliverance; others read it as Astra abandoning them.

### Option 2 — The Almanac Year *(farmers, moons, and the two suns)*

Daily life keys to the sky's slow machinery rather than the strikes. An almanac culture.

Everyday texture:

- People ask "which moons are up," not "what's the date." Maru full = sow; Lethe waxing = the estuary fish run.
- The rare night all three moons rise together is **Trithyr** — a harvest-festival of bonfires and matchmaking that anchors the social year.
- Children are named for the sky they were born under: a "Veska-child" is quiet and lucky; a "two-sun child" is bold and trouble.
- Seasons run hot-and-fast or long-and-mild depending on the suns' overlap that year, so almanacs are precious hand-copied heirlooms and a village sky-reader is valued like a healer.
- Strikes are treated as *weather* — a hazard read alongside the moons ("don't travel near a low Ossin").

Fallibility hook: the moons quietly drift over a lifetime, and the decade-Showerings have *never once* matched the predicted year. A generation's heirloom almanac has gone subtly, dangerously wrong — festivals land on bad days, sowing fails — and no one knows why.

### Option 3 — The Sky That Lies *(omens, charms, and birth-signs)*

The Earth-standard count is underneath, but nobody *experiences* it as numbers — they experience it as signs. Year-0 event: **the First Gleaming** (the impact remembered as the sky first speaking).

Everyday texture:

- Daily life runs on read-the-sky superstition: don't leave home when Veska rises blood-pale; tie a glass charm (cheap Whatwill dust) over the cradle when Ossin is low.
- Every household has its own half-inherited, half-invented sky-lore.
- **Birth-signs are destiny** — the configuration you're born under decides who you marry, what trade you're steered toward, whether the village trusts you. A child born during a Keening is blessed or doomed by local creed.
- The whole Cosmic Ladder is understood as *the sky keeping promises* — comfort comes from the rhythm. The world is terrifying but legible.

Fallibility hook: the sky breaks a promise — a Keening no sign foretold, or a "guaranteed" Glistening-omen that passes with nothing. To this culture that's not a calendar error, it's the cosmos *lying* — apocalypse-grade theology. The population is spiritually unmoored: some abandon the charms, some double down, some hunt for whoever "broke the sky."

## How They Combine

These are not mutually exclusive. The skeleton + Cosmic Ladder + sky bodies are shared canon. The three options are really **three regional/cultural lenses** on the same world:

- A strike-battered frontier reckons time by the falls (Option 1).
- An agrarian heartland lives by the almanac (Option 2).
- A devout or remote culture reads the sky for omens (Option 3).

The group can pick one as the **dominant** everyday culture and keep the others as minority/regional flavor — which also satisfies the worldbuilding rule that isolated landmasses develop divergent cultures.
