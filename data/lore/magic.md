Magic is rare. Those who wield it are rarer still.

## How Magic Works

- Source: standard D&D 5e cosmology, played straight and deep — wizards study the Weave, sorcerers are born to it, warlocks make pacts. Arcane, divine, primal, and psionic are distinct forces.
- Public perception tracks the real distinctions: common people know a guild wizard from a temple priest from a druid of the wilds. A respectable wizard is a very different thing from a respectable priest — different institutions, different fears, different courtesies. (How the faiths view the arcane belongs to [[World Religion]].)

## Rarity in Practice

Magic is rare (~3/10). Presence is significant, not routine. Creates hard social stratification.

- **What magic uniquely provides:** true medicine — without a caster, people rely on herbs and time. Long-distance communication runs on carrier pigeons and horseback couriers; the world's tech range *may* brush against magic-level communication at its upper edge, but it is never routine.
- **Witnessing magic:** depends on conditioning. Dense populations are somewhat accustomed; small towns barely understand it and meet it with awe. Either way it marks the caster as *above* — closer to legend, or even minor divinity, than to neighbor.
- **Social position:** magical ability usually confers power. It reads as anywhere between ruthless dominance and a charity to the world, depending on the wielder and the witness. Some populations run the other way entirely — adverse, even hateful, toward magic users.

## The Pyramid

Magic is a pyramid: a few at the top shape ages, many in the middle build the world, some at the bottom make the people laugh — or curse.

This shape is universal — every tradition thins toward its peak. The circles have their archdruids, the covens their matriarchs, each as rare at the summit as any arch mage. But only the wizards formalized their pyramid into institutions, and theirs is the one articulated below. The other traditions' pyramids remain unwritten until their own lore fills in.

### High, Grand & Arch Mages — extremely rare

Guardians of the deepest mysteries.

- Precious few high mages exist in the mortal world. Grand mages are scarcer still. Arch mages are rarer than a polite argument in a dwarfish tavern.
- Reality-benders, rulers, war-makers, age-shapers. They live for centuries — rarity is their inventory — and they do not go quietly.
- In calm ages, the appearance of a single new arch mage in a generation is cause for wonder. But in *interesting times, there are many* — arch mages cluster in turbulent eras, and those clusters are the epic chapters of [[World History]]. Whether the crisis raises the mages or the mages raise the crisis is never quite settled.
- Which arch mages exist now, and where: DM-controlled. Establish in play, not here.

### Working Wizards — uncommon but essential

Unrecorded. Uncelebrated. Indispensable.

- Most never seek immortality or ultimate mysteries — they make themselves useful. Hired by shipyards, merchant fleets, kingdoms, and guilds.
- Their work: construction and engineering (aqueducts, fortresses, bridges), shipbuilding and navigation, military engineering and battle magic, architecture and city planning, guild enchanting and industry, improving harvests, trade, and daily life.
- This is the guild rank-and-file — and the world's decisive military asset. Combined rituals (many mages casting the same ultra-spell over extended time) make warfare brutally asymmetric against any side that fields no mages.
- For every arch mage in the history books, dozens of working wizards stood beside the people.

### Hedge Wizards & Magicians — common enough to be called magicians

Entertainers, fixers, and folk of uncertain standing.

- Talents are modest but varied. Known as "magicians" by those who wish to be accurate.
- They work in market squares, back alleys, and village roofs: entertaining crowds, fixing leaky roofs, finding (or causing) lost purses, small tricks and petty works of spellcraft.
- Regarded as somewhere between tradesmen and nuisances. Respectability vanishes the moment something pedestrian needs fixing.
- This is the tier that lives outside guild sanction — see **Outside the Guilds**.

## The Guilds

Wizard guilds operate as cartels — controlling lineages, enforcing codes, posting prices. Members recognizable; freelancers viewed with suspicion. Philosophically distinct institutions that compete through precedence rather than open conflict.

- **Origin:** independent formation. Each guild arose separately, shaped by the lore and culture of its region — each approaches magic and philosophy differently.
- **Character:** guilds are characterized by broad focuses — politics, aerodynamics, mining, spirituality — that color everything they do.
- **Member recognition:** guild members are imperious, and each tries to embody the general characteristics of their guild — or is at least aware of and supportive of them. There is variety even within a guild; members have personalities, not uniforms.
- **Warfare:** when guilds fight, or when kingdoms field them, the decisive factor is combined ritual casting — many mages, one ultra-spell, more time. A war with mage guilds on one side only is not a war; it is an unveiling.

## Outside the Guilds

- Other traditions build structures too — druids have their groves, sorcerers their covens, and some grow formidable. But only the wizard guilds built for *visibility and force projection*: certification, posted prices, registries, enforcement arms. The guilds are the most seen and the most able to project power across a region — which is not the same as being the most powerful *in* a given region, where an older grove or coven may quietly outmatch them.
- **Fate of unlicensed casters:** varies by guild. The most extreme guilds aggressively police who is "certified" — mage police, registries, enforcement. The least extreme run light outreach and relations, holding only that powerful spellcasters in an area *should be known*.
- The hedge tier survives in the gap between those policies — beneath guild notice in some regions, hunted in others.

## Traditions in Relation

- **Arcane vs. divine:** publicly and institutionally distinct. Respectability in one buys nothing in the other.
- **Natural/shamanic tradition:** present and real — groves, covens, and older ways — but informal by nature. Their summits are as rare as the wizards', and far less recorded.

## Shadows

*Load-bearing unknowns. Do not resolve here — establish in play.*

- Objects and structures that behave like magic but follow no known rules exist. No faction controls or understands them. They feel wrong, not evil. Lost high technology can produce effects indistinguishable from spellwork — rarity and introduction follow the party's Remnant Tech spec (`meta/worldbuilding.md`), one arc element at a time.
- At least one actor in the world treats magical power as something to be possessed rather than wielded. Details DM-controlled.
