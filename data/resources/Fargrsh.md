# Fargrsh

One of the [[Alchemical Herbs]] circulating through the world's underside — the only one with a doctrine attached.

## Names

- **Fargrsh** — the real name, used in proper company. Said with a strong guttural push.
- **Witchweed** — the folktale name. Cutesy, softened for children's stories; far gentler than the real thing.
- **Rea** — what the streets have taken to calling it.
- **The Havenot** — what those who truly dabble call it. Simple enough to describe exactly what it does.

## Appearance

Surprisingly adept at camouflage: the plant takes on the characteristics of whatever grows around it. But it always tastes like the complete opposite of what it's near — as if it knows it's corrupting, and can hide its foul intent in looks but never in taste. Those who know the identifying markers can spot it on sight.

> Identifying markers are deliberately undefined — DM elaborates in play.

## Effects

Fargrsh gives what you do not have — that's why people enjoy it so much.

- **The poor and starving** feel full and live, briefly, in high society. The comedown is terrible: empty stomachs and crushing reality drive abuse until death, usually by whatever means the drug convinced them they were satiated in.
- **The rich and powerful** get glimpses of empathy and true love outside the bounds they know. Few can name the emotions they're having; most use Fargrsh as a casual party drug and waste the glimpse.
- **Under the right tutelage** it has been used in ceremonies for great good — but not everyone can handle it, and not everyone can guide it.

## Where It Grows

It can grow anywhere, but the ground must be *used* — it cannot take root where nothing has tread, as if it needs the presence of spirits. It appears in graveyards, but only well-walked ones. The more desolate a location, the rarer it is, which makes covert cultivation nearly impossible without raising suspicion among those who know. For all its camouflage and shadow-market existence, the plant almost seems to want to be recognised and out in the open.

## Who Knows

Talked about in fairy tales, often as comedic relief about the dangers of addiction — kept alive by groups like the **Modra**, household matriarchs shielding their youth from the reality of life.

The true supply is controlled by [[The Deorful Domas]], who move like the plant itself: the beggar on the corner or the judge in the courtroom.
