At the heart of the central forest of **the Marrow Concord** stands **Orrowan** — a demigod in the shape of a colossal ancient tree, asleep since before memory. Each of the three peoples names it by their bond to it: the elves call it **the Dawnroot**, for the waking they tend toward; the human settlers of Solenne call it **the Godwood**, warily, from the outside; and the lizardfolk call it **Orro'ssha**, the elder first-tongue word the name *Orrowan* was ever only a smoothing of. Its dreams leak into the wood as the forest's living magic; where its roots run, the land is greener, older, and half-awake itself. It is not a watchful god. It is a sleeping one — and unlike most sleeping powers, its waking is the thing its people *want*.

**The Orrow** — the elves of the middle woodland — are its current keepers and its wakers. Their whole culture is a patient project: keep the forest whole and growing, because a whole forest is the condition of the god's rising, and a rising Orrowan means renewal for the wood, the elves, perhaps the whole Concord. This is why the Orrow cannot yield ground to Solenne's settlers. Every acre the coast clears is not merely trespass — it pushes the Deep Dreamer one step further from waking. To the Orrow it is deicide by delay.

But the name **Orrowan** is older than the elves. It comes from **the Sesh'kar** — the lizardfolk of the deep Marrow, who knew and named it before the Orrow ever walked the wood. To them it is Orro'ssha, and always was. They hold the older understanding of it, and perhaps the missing piece of how it wakes. Between the two forest peoples runs a quiet, unsettled question: the Orrow keep the god, but the Sesh'kar named it — so whose god is it?

Why Orrowan still sleeps is not yet known — a forest not yet whole, an old wound unhealed, a cycle not yet come, or something the Sesh'kar remember and the Orrow have forgotten. Jeff's contribution — the demigod at the center of the Marrow Concord's forest, seeding the Orrow and the Sesh'kar and the faith that binds them.
