The Orrow are the elves of the middle woodland belt — the central-forest power of **[[The Marrow Concord]]**, and the buffer everyone else has to cross. Their whole culture is a single long, hopeful project: to bring their god to waking. That god is **[[Orrowan]]**, the sleeping demigod at the forest's heart, which they call **the Dawnroot** — and its waking, they believe, would be renewal for the forest, for the elves, perhaps for the whole Concord.

Everything follows from that hope. Because the forest's wholeness is the condition of the waking, tending the forest *is* devotion, and harm to the forest is harm to the god. This is why the Orrow read human logging the way no settler intends it: not as trespass on some acres of timber, but as **deicide by delay** — every cleared stand pushing Orrowan further from the dawn. It is why they cannot simply cede ground to Solenne's growing hunger, and why the friction between them and the settlers is not a negotiable border dispute but a difference about what the forest even is.

Their devotion has an outer form and an inner secret. Outwardly they are the visible clergy of **[[The Circle of Orro'ssha]]**, the druid order at the tree — the dream-readers and forest-shapers, the numbers and the daily rites. But those rites were founded by the **[[The Sesh'kar]]**, the lizardfolk who knew Orrowan first, and the Orrow perform much of that inherited liturgy earnestly without fully grasping it. The Orrow believe they are the wakers. That belief sits atop an unspoken question — whether they are truly Orrowan's people, or only its most recent ones — and that question has not yet come due.
