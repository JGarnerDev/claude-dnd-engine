The Grey Mothers are not an order. They have no head, no hall, no roster — only the eldest woman of each plains village on the [[Serhari]] margin, tending the patch her mothers tended, reading the year to know whether it will seed, and holding the lore of the harvest and the two preparations in living memory alone. It is never written. It passes grandmother to granddaughter, and a village that loses its Mother before she has passed it on loses its patch.

This is why the wizard guild that holds the Serhari harvest contract has never actually controlled the plant. There is no gate to storm — the "gate" is a thousand quiet households, and the guild must court each one and mostly fails. A distant patron can buy a paper from the plains government; only a Grey Mother decides what leaves her patch, and most decide *no*.

So the plains carry two authorities. The landed families and village confederation govern grain and politics and trade the region's food freely. The Grey Mothers govern **Serhari** — an older, quieter power the government does not command. Not a gram of Vaelthari moves without the grandmothers' consent, and a Mother who breaks ranks to sell the white into open markets is not just breaking a contract but a taboo; the plains have long memories.

Their strength is entirely soft — patience, secrecy, cultural weight, and the simple fact that they cannot be bought all at once. It is also their whole vulnerability. They are unarmed elders. The day a guild, or a hungry neighbor, stops being patient, soft power meets hard, and the Grey Mothers have nothing but the plains' regard to shield them.
