The Thelven are the people of the eastern forest's interior — wood elves in plurality, humans in a near-equal share, firbolg and other forest-kin the rest. They are the only people in the basin among whom humans are not the majority, and among the few anywhere who would find the observation uninteresting.

## Membership, not ground

Everything about them follows from one fact: they reckon belonging by **who answers a summons**, not by where a line falls. A band inside the eastern league's territory that sends a speaker to the wood-court is Thelven; a stranger living in the deepest interior is not. This is not a philosophy they argue for. It is simply how the question has always been asked, and the fact that outsiders ask it differently strikes them as an outsider problem.

The practical result is a people who are comfortably dual-belonging at the margin — paying league fees the way one pays for anything, and walking east when the court gathers — and who have never had to notice that those two loyalties could be made to conflict.

## Patience as a procedure

A full sitting of the court takes a season to gather. Thelven do not experience this as delay. Among them, a matter that waited has been *deliberated*; a matter settled quickly has been settled by whoever happened to be standing there, which is fine for small things and suspect for large ones. Merchants at the posts read this as obstruction or stupidity. It is neither, and the misreading is durable because neither side has any incentive to correct it.

The same instinct governs internal life: no band tells another how to live, and a speaker who came home from the court with a ruling about a band's own affairs would be laughed at. Authority points outward only.

## The courtesy

Something inward is owed a courtesy. The Thelven pay it, and they do not talk about it where an outsider can hear — not from secrecy exactly, but from the same reflex that keeps a family's arrangements out of a shopkeeper's ear. There are places the guides will not go, and the refusals are old enough to have become etiquette rather than fear. Bands nearest **[[The Inward]]** send speakers most reliably and say least about why.

What is owed, and whether the courtesy is a bargain, a tribute, or a habit whose reason has been forgotten — none of that is settled here.

## Not a faith

It is worth saying plainly what the Thelven are *not*, because the basin's other forest people are otherwise. The Thelven have no god in the wood, no waking to tend, no liturgy. Their forest is not sacred; it is simply theirs. They will fight for it the way anyone fights for a home, not the way anyone fights for a temple.

This is why [[Whatwill]] moves through their hands unremarked — mixed into fur and timber loads at the posts, sold as an ordinary forest good at ordinary forest prices, by people who have built no theology around fallen sky and see no reason to start.
