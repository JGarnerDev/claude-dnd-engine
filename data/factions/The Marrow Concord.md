The Marrow Concord is the young nation of the southern reach — forest-and-river country behind the bay's south shore, half-settled and growing. Its name is the thing it is: an accord binding three unlike peoples, and **the Marrow**, the deep tidal river that runs down out of the elvish forest, through the lizardfolk depths, and out to the human coast — the shared artery that makes three domains one country. Outsiders call it **the Concord**.

It has no single ruler. It is a balance of three powers, each holding its own ground and meeting the others only at the seams:

- **[[Solenne]]**, the human settler port on the warm south shore — the sea-trading leg, the one that swells every season with new arrivals, and the only one that faces Cyan's south jaw and hates the toll it pays there.
- **[[The Orrow]]**, the elves of the middle woodland belt — the buffer everyone crosses, keepers of the long project of waking **[[Orrowan]]**, the sleeping demigod at the forest's heart.
- **[[The Sesh'kar]]**, the reclusive lizardfolk of the deep river-forest and swamp — Orrowan's first people, holding an older understanding of the tree than the elves who now front its worship through **[[The Circle of Orro'ssha]]**.

What holds them together is parity — no leg strong enough to swallow the others — plus interdependence and the shared weight of outside powers pressing on all three. What threatens them is that the parity is slipping. Solenne grows and grows; its land-hunger drives settlers inland, and inland is not empty — it is the other two powers' homeland. Every acre the settlers clear is, to the Orrow, a delay in their god's waking, which is to say a small deicide. Meanwhile the fight the coast most wants — breaking Cyan's strait toll, seizing the south jaw — is a fight the forest and the depths do not share, so pursuing it means one third of the nation dragging the other two toward a war they have no stake in. And beneath all of it runs the oldest crack: the Orrow and the Sesh'kar worship the same sleeping god and quietly disagree about whose god it is.
