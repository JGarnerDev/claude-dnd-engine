Nadirium is the one arcane body in Vaskgrath shaped like a school rather than a cartel. Where the region's guild-mages are neutral hands for hire — buy an effect, pay the fee, part ways — the Listeners are a craft with apprentices, a body of method, and a subject they will not put down: the deep stone of **[[Orngrath]]**, and what it remembers.

### The principle

Their discipline rests on one observed fact, which they hold to be as physical as ore assay and no more mystical than it needs to be: **dense, old, enclosed stone keeps a record.** What passes through deep rock — presences, movements, the shape of events pressed against it over long spans — leaves a faint standing resonance in the stone, the way a struck bell keeps ringing below hearing. Shallow rock loses it. Open or worked rock loses it. But the oldest holds, cut deep and then sealed, are near-perfect keepers: dark, still, undisturbed, they hold their resonance for ages. A Reader does not summon anything and does not foretell anything. A Reader *recovers* what the stone already holds — reads the past out of the rock like an assayer reads metal out of ore.

This is why the sealed dead holds are the School's central subject and its central prize. They are not tombs to Nadirium; they are the densest archives in the world, and the thing every Reader most wants to recover is the last thing those holds recorded: what moved through them, and what emptied them.

### The method and its instruments

The craft is slow, physical, and unglamorous. A Reader descends, alone or with an apprentice, into a hold and goes completely still — light out, breath low — because their own presence muddies the read the way footprints muddy a track. Between the Reader and the stone sit the instruments the miners find unsettling: **black glass and bone.** Both are receptive media. Volcanic black glass, drawn from Orngrath's own deep seams, takes the stone's resonance cleanly; worked bone tunes and steadies it, the way a soundbox shapes a string. The Reader learns, over years, to hold the instruments against live stone and let what the rock keeps register through them — first as pattern, then, with training, as something a trained ear can actually parse. Miners who watch call it *listening*, and the name stuck: **the Listeners.** The School prefers *reading*, because listening implies a voice, and the stone has none. It only keeps.

### What it can and cannot do

The rigor of the School is in its honesty about limits, and a Reader who oversells a read is disgraced:

- It recovers **presence and event, not intent** — that something large and slow passed, not what it wanted.
- It is **directional and partial** — stronger toward what pressed hardest and longest against the rock, near-silent on anything brief or gentle.
- It **degrades with depth of time** — recent record reads clear; the truly ancient reads as pressure and shape without detail.
- It **cannot cross a break** — a collapse, a fresh cut, or a blast wipes the resonance past it, which is exactly why the School fights the bosses over blasting a hold before it is read.

### The cost

Nadirium does not pretend the work is free, but it frames the cost as a trade condition, not a curse — closer to a smith's ruined hearing or a miner's grey lung than anything sinister. Long, deep listening dulls a Reader to ordinary sound and quickens their attention to the very low and very slow; veteran Readers grow quiet, unhurried, and a little remote in company, tending to answer a beat late, as if part of them is still down in the stillness waiting for the stone to resolve. Apprentices are warned plainly. Most accept it the way any tradesman accepts what the trade takes. A few leave. The School records the condition, studies it, and has not found a way around it — which some outsiders read as candor and others as a discipline that has simply gotten used to what it does to people.

### Standing and relations

Vaskgrath treats Nadirium roughly the way a town treats its coroner: indispensable, respected, and kept a step back. The human **town-bosses** need them — no one else can tell a shaft crew whether a sealed hold still holds workable metal, and the arms buildup wants that metal badly. The **dwarf hold-lords** are the friction: they sealed those holds deliberately, they have never fully said why, and they distrust anyone whose whole purpose is to reopen the question. The School's problem is that its own scholarly hunger to *know* what emptied the holds and the bosses' plain hunger for the ore both push the same way — toward opening — while the dwarves, and a quiet minority of Readers, argue that a thing worth sealing that thoroughly might be worth leaving read-but-shut.

In all this the Listeners are the near-mirror of Jeff's other native arcane order, **[[The Circle of Orro'ssha]]** in the southern forest: both are rooted disciplines rather than cartels, both grew out of the ground they study, both stand as the local answer to the mercenary guilds. One keeps vigil over what sleeps; the other reads what has already gone. Neither is for sale.
