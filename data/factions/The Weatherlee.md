The storm-battered outer west coast of [[Solmara]] — the only ocean shore that isn't Red's port, screened from everyone by the [[Ardenwall]] and reachable in practice only by sea. Winter closes the harbor for months; poverty and weather make the lee-folk tough, insular, and willing to raid when the fishing fails. They sit on two things worth killing for: the cliffs where [[Stormglass]] forms, and the one door into the region that pays no strait toll — the back door that moves [[Coffee]], glass, and contraband past the wharf fees entirely. Their problem is that everything they have is worth exactly as much as their obscurity.

## Name

They named themselves for shelter they don't have. *Lee* is the sheltered side, the calm water behind the land — and there is no lee on the storm coast, which is the joke, and they know it's the joke, and they keep the name anyway. Outsiders who don't get it think the Weatherlee must be a soft green harbor somewhere. That misunderstanding is the first line of defense: nobody sails looking for a hard place with a soft name.

## Government

Too poor and too scattered for a standing seat. Each cove is its own village under whoever owns the biggest boats; between them, nothing — until the sea makes a decision no single cove can answer, and then the captains gather and speak a **Tide-Word**: a binding call for one season only. Raid or don't. Trade with this ship or sink it. Tell the outsiders this story about the cliffs. The Tide-Word dissolves when the season turns, and a captain who tried to make one last is put back in his boat. The seat, such as it is, sits at [[Storm Coast Harbor]].

Nominally they are Red's — the [[Highland Seat]]'s maps color the storm coast as a far Reach holding, and [[The Ardvellyn Reach]] has never once garrisoned it because the weather does the garrisoning for free. The lee-folk let Red keep the color on the map. It costs them nothing and keeps Red from wondering why the coast isn't worth taking. Independent in every way that matters; a province in exactly the way that's cheapest to allow.

## The cliffs

What the lee-folk tell outsiders is that the storm cliffs are haunted — that the drowned go *up* instead of down, that the [[Stormglass]] is the drowned made brittle, and to quarry it is to be taken next storm. They tell it flatly, without selling it, the way you tell true things. It works because it's half-true from the inside: people *do* die on the cliffs harvesting glass in the storms it takes to form, and the lee-folk have simply declined to correct the outsider's conclusion about *why*.

*Unresolved:* whether the haunting is only a good lie, or whether generations of dying on the same cliffs for the same glass have made it, by now, a little bit true.

*Twist option left live:* **Green as storm-blooded** — goliath stock or storm-touched genasi in the oldest cove families, giving the Stormglass a "the cliffs chose us" dimension. Written weathered and flat so the storm-blood can be the *deepest* families, switched on later without rewriting anyone.

*DM note:* like the rest of the basin, the Lee runs uniformly grim and flat. When running Green, break the cold once — a cove wedding weathered-in for a week, a captain who laughs, a Tide-Word gathering that turns to a feast. A coast players will want to return to needs one bright spot against the storm, and one clear ally-face among the raiders.
