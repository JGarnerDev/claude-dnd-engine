The Thelvenwood is the deep interior of the eastern forest, and the word is a people first and a territory second — which is the whole problem for anyone drawing borders. Ask a Thelven where the Thelvenwood ends and you get an answer about who lives there, not where the line runs. The league's clerks write *Thelvenwood* on the tally sheets and mean a blot on a map; the Thelven mean everyone who answers a summons to the court. Those are not the same set, and the difference is roughly a hundred thousand people nobody has agreed how to count.

Nobody has counted the rest either. Somewhere near four hundred thousand, by the only estimates that exist, all of which are the league's. Wood elves are the plurality, humans a near-equal share, firbolg and other forest-kin the remainder — the one nation on this map where humans are not the majority.

## The wood-court

A real seat with real authority, deliberately hard to reach. It sits somewhere in **[[The Inward]]** at a place the Thelven do not name to outsiders; the tracks are unmarked and the last two days of the approach run through country that requires a guide who has to *agree* to guide you.

Its authority runs over people, not ground. It can deliberate, bind itself, and keep an agreement — which makes it far more dangerous to the bay powers than a scatter of kin-bands would be. It also cannot defend a line, project force, or do anything on a merchant's timescale. Treat it as a nation with the axes rotated: strong where states are weak, weak where states are strong. It cannot meaningfully be invaded, because there is no capital to take and taking ground does not take people.

## The league, and what it thinks it is buying

The Thelven were holding court under the same canopy before the first post at the treeline was a stockade. From the inside, the eastern timber league is a recent encrustation on the forest's edge — a thing that grew up in a couple of centuries and will presumably come off in a couple more, the way such things do. They sell through the posts because the posts happen to be there. There is no grievance in it, which is exactly what unsettles the factors: you cannot buy off contempt with a better rate.

The gap that makes it playable:

- **The league believes** it is dealing with a backward supplier dependent on it for market access, and prices accordingly. The margin it takes is understood, on its side, as the price of civilization.
- **The Thelven believe** they are humoring a tenant. They tolerate the posts the way you tolerate weather.
- **Neither has tested it.** Nobody in the league has asked what happens if the wood stops selling for one season; nobody in the court has asked what the bands would actually do if the court told them to.

## The fault line nobody has mapped

The basin's political map is drawn in territory. The forest-kin organize in membership. A wood elf band at the league's forest margin is inside league jurisdiction on any chart and inside the Thelvenwood by its own reckoning, and sees no contradiction — it pays the league's fees the way it pays for anything, and sends a speaker east when the court gathers.

So the margin forest-kin are dual-belonging rather than subject. The league's rule over them is real for taxation and unreal for loyalty, and it has never had cause to discover the difference. The court's actual reach extends invisibly into league territory; nobody has mapped it because the mapping convention cannot hold it.

Any bay power that forces a choice — a loyalty oath, a conscription, a border closure — converts a century of comfortable ambiguity into a defection overnight. It is the region's quiet fault line, and it is cheap to trip.

## What it looks like from inside, at three depths

The court exists to keep these three from concluding different things loudly enough to matter:

- **The margin**, trading at the posts, sees trading partners and increasingly sees a bad deal — the only Thelven who know what things sell for on the far side.
- **The interior** sees weather. Bay politics happen downhill and wash out before arriving.
- **The deepest bands** do not see the bay powers at all. They see what is behind them.
