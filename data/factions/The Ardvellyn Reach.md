Peninsula highland realm on [[Solmara]], holding the spine of the [[Ardenwall]] and one ocean-facing port at [[Outer-Coast Port]]. The Reachfolk live off stone, ore, and wool from hard high country, and off the fees they take on every coffee cargo that lands at their wharf. Proud, guarded, comfortable defending high ground — weak the moment they step off the peninsula.

## Name

Named for the ridge, not the coast, which is the whole quarrel in a word. "Reach" means the high country — the long grasp of the Ardenwall down to the sea — and the Reachfolk say it to mean *everything the ridge overlooks*, coast included. The coast-towns hear the same word and understand it to stop where the rock stops. Nobody has drawn the line, because drawing it would decide the argument, and both sides would rather keep the argument than lose it.

## Government

The [[Highland Seat]] holds a crown that is really a chairmanship, granted by the Ridge-Council. The council — mining clans (dwarven, mostly) and wool-lords of the high valleys (human, mostly) — sends one voice per holding, weighted by what each can withhold. A clan that controls a pass the army needs speaks louder than its numbers.

- **The Seat can** speak for the Reach outward: treat, war, set the coast-fees, garrison the passes.
- **The Seat cannot** open a vein, move a clan, or override a holding on its own ground. Try to tax a clan's ore directly and the pass above the capital quietly closes.
- **Seat vs. Port:** the Seat stays in the mountains at (8) by design. To rule from the wharf at (28) would be to admit the coast is the country — so the crown stays on the rock and sends down a governor it can afford to lose.

An outsider notices the custom at once: you do not carry a drawn or ready weapon through a Reach pass. A bared blade on the high road is read as *insult* — a claim you don't trust the ridge to keep its own road safe. The coast-towns don't observe this at all, and the Reachfolk cite that as proof the coast was never really theirs.

## The coast claim

Red's claim to the peninsula coast is old, written, and completely untested below the tide-line. The coast-towns pay the landing-fees at [[Outer-Coast Port]] because the Port is Red's — that part is uncontested. What they reject is everything past the wharf: the levy, the muster, the right to name their harbor-masters. For a hundred years neither side has forced it, because the Reach can't project force off the peninsula and the coast can't do without the Port. It's a claim kept alive by never being cashed. [[The Weatherlee]] is the far end of that same claim — the piece of coast that proves the Reach cannot enforce it.

## The coffee gateway

[[Coffee]] lands first at [[Outer-Coast Port]] off the southwest ocean lane, so Red imports and re-ships more of it than anyone. The Reach takes a landing-fee, a warehouse-fee, and a re-ship-fee — and it reads as wealth from the outside. From the inside the Reachfolk know exactly what they are: the most expensive stretch of road on the coffee road, and roads don't set the price of what rolls over them. Whether the factor-house at the Port is a guest or a garrison is unsettled — the Houses run the bean; Red only runs the wharf it has to cross.

*Unresolved:* whether the strait forts answer to the Seat, hold for the coast-towns, or quietly hold for themselves.

*Twist option left live:* **Red as a dwarven realm proper** — flip the ratio, dwarves the majority, the Ridge-Council a clan-moot, the peninsula claim an ancestral-stone claim. Written human-led with a strong dwarven ore-bloc so the twist stays a door.

*DM note:* the whole basin reads one temperature — dry, bitter, withholding, cold stone and cold water. Great prose, but monochrome at the table. When running Red, plant a warm counterweight: a loud generous clan-head, a passes-garrison festival, humor in the wool valleys. Give the party at least one Reachfolk face to *like* against all the resentment.
