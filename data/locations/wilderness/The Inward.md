**The Inward** is what the forest margin calls the country behind it. It is not a name the deepest bands use, or at least not one they use where an outsider can hear, and it is not on any chart — the eastern league's maps show the forest fading into an unhatched blank a few days past the treeline posts, which is an honest depiction of what its surveyors know.

Somewhere in it sits the wood-court of **[[The Thelvenwood]]** — a real seat, genuinely reachable. The tracks are unmarked and the last two days of the approach run through country requiring a guide who has to *agree* to guide you. That consent is the actual gate; there is no wall, no watch, and no one who will stop a determined traveler from walking in and not coming out.

Past the court, the forest keeps going, and the character of the place changes. There are stands the guides will not enter. The refusals are not delivered as warnings — they are delivered the way one declines an invitation, politely, without explanation, and without any possibility of being argued out of. They are old enough to have settled into etiquette. **[[The Thelven]]** bands nearest this country send speakers to the court most reliably of any in the wood, and volunteer the least about their reasons.

Something in here is owed a courtesy, and it is paid.

Along the outer margin, where the trees thin toward the plains frontier, lies the edge of the [[Whatwill]] debris field. Sky-glass works its way out of the wood in fur and timber loads, handled by people with no particular opinion about it. Whether the courtesy paid deeper in has anything to do with a field of fallen sky is not a question anyone at the treeline has thought to ask.

*DM note: what is actually back here is deliberately unwritten. The Thelven material establishes only that a courtesy exists and that it predates anyone's memory of its reason.*
