# Campaign Design Preferences

Player-sourced design desires. Not world facts — what the group wants to *experience*. Surface when the story creates a fitting opportunity. Don't force; don't bury.

**Deployment tracking:** each item has a *Deployed:* line. Update it with a wikilink to the session or mission where it fired. `—` means not yet used. At act transitions, check what's aging out and seed it into the next arc.

**Seeding:** an item can be *seeded* — a deliberate breadcrumb placed in play (a detail, a rumor, a banded crow) without the item itself firing. Add a *Seeded:* line under the item with a wikilink to the session and a one-phrase note of what the breadcrumb was. Seeded items are not deployed: the aging check should treat them as alive, not stalled, and session planning should know the breadcrumb exists before placing another.

**Cross-campaign seeding is explicitly allowed:** most items here target the homebrew world, but a breadcrumb may be planted during the Strahd campaign for a payoff after the transition. A seed planted in Barovia that pays off in the new world is a strong transition stitch — `/session` may suggest one when the story offers a natural opening.

**Aging cadence:** the aging check runs every 5th played session (`todo-brief.ps1` prints an AGING CHECK line) and at act transitions. Review undeployed items; seed or consciously retire what's stalling.

## Desired Major Events

Story beats the group wants — as instigators, arc consequences, or background crises. Specifics (who, where, when) are DM-controlled.

- **Catastrophe during peace** — something terrible happens on a happy day: a festival, a wedding, a moment of genuine relief. The contrast is the point. Could be political assassination, magical detonation, or invasion. The world changes in a session.
  - *Deployed: —*
- **Politically destabilizing love affair** — a romance between figures whose relationship is forbidden or catastrophic for power structures. Lovers could be nobles, cross-faction leaders, or royals. What breaks: alliances, succession lines, wars. Details DM-controlled.
  - *Deployed: —*
- **Plague** — disease as instigator or side effect of a major arc. Strains factions, creates desperation, shifts who has power. Distinct event, not background noise.
  - *Deployed: —*
- **Pestilence / crop failure** — one or more key crops ruined; food scarcity reshapes regional politics. Separate from plague. The two can chain.

## Antagonist Archetypes

Design inspiration. Suggest when an opportunity fits — not a checklist to exhaust.

- **Snakeoil salesman** — charismatic deceiver. Sells false cures, promises, or power. Hard to stop because people want to believe him.

- **Mercenary wizard guild controlling crows** — organized arcane cartel with a surveillance network. Crows are the visible arm; what else they control is unknown. Variant of the wizard guild texture in [[worldbuilding]] — this one is for-hire, secretive, embedded in cities.
  - *Deployed: —*
- **Large insectoids** — alien logic, hive behavior, hard to communicate with. Not mindless — *wrong*. Their goals are coherent from their own frame.
  - *Deployed: —*
- **Giant walking machine** — construct or war machine of overwhelming scale. Fits the remnant tech / steampunk ceiling in [[worldbuilding]]. The question is who built it and whether it can be stopped or redirected.

- **Fantasy Borg** — hivemind collective. Assimilation-focused. What it absorbs it becomes. Not evil in its own frame — different.
  - *Deployed: —*
- **Sephiroth type** — beautiful tragic villain with god complex. Once good, or once understandable. Corrupted by revelation or loss. Compelling, not cartoonish.

## Mission Preferences

Structures the group enjoys. Use as a palette, not a formula.

- **Ocean's 11 heist** — the plan is a character; each crew member has a role and failure is the wrong person doing the wrong job. The mark is formidable — guarded, protected, or both. Reconnaissance is its own session. Information asymmetry is the tension: the crew knows things the mark doesn't, and vice versa. The plan breaks; the crew adapts. A twist reveal pays off something seeded earlier. Violence is a failure state — elegance over force. Stakes beyond the score: getting caught has real consequences.
  - *Deployed: —*
- **Kill Bill revenge** — betrayal as origin; named targets on a list, crossed off one by one. Each target is a chapter, each formidable. The quarry knows you're coming — they prepare, flee, or face it. Backstory assembles in reverse as you hunt. Underdog momentum: less power, more rage. Some targets may be sympathetic; the revenge doesn't care. The final confrontation earns its weight from everything before it.
  - *Deployed: —*
- **Court cases** — social and political; evidence is a resource, gathered before the session and deployed in argument. NPC jury or panel with motives. Corrupt judges, false witnesses, and procedural traps are obstacles. The party may be prosecuting, defending, or on trial themselves. High roleplay density; preparation matters as much as performance.
  - *Deployed: —*
- **Assassinations and guerrilla warfare** — occupied or oppressed territory; the party as underground resistance fighting a force they can't beat head-on. Sabotage, targeted elimination, operating in the shadows of a dominant power. The enemy controls the streets; the party controls the cracks.
  - *Deployed: —*
- **Gorilla warfare** — literal gorillas *with* guerrilla tactics. Deliberate. Comedic chaos is the point. The gorillas introduce themselves verbatim as "gorilla guerillas" (though they have a different name for their faction) after intense lore building. Tonal release valve — not a failure of tone, but a conscious break from it.
  - *Deployed: —*

## Session Preferences

- No hard limitations on session type
- All pillars welcome: combat, roleplay, crafting, mystery — mix freely
- Variety over formula

## Player Agency Principle

Party and player choices carry above-average weight in the world. Their actions move things. Don't overdo — they are consequential, not omnipotent. The world reacts; it doesn't always comply.
