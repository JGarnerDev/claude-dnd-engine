# Monk Graduations

**Projection: absence.** Monastic institutions project *withdrawal* — mountains famous for what won't come down from them. Where every other institution advertises what it can do, a monastery's reputation is built entirely on refusal: closed gates, unanswered envoys, and the occasional graduate who redefines what a person is.

### Grandmasters & Perfected Ones — extremely rare

More rumor than person.

- Grandmasters of the high monasteries, living techniques, the perfected ones said to have stopped aging out of politeness. Most famous ones may already be dead; with this tier it is genuinely hard to tell, and the monasteries do not clarify.
- They shape ages by *teaching* — one grandmaster's students, two generations later, are a martial tradition, a bodyguard lineage, and a heresy. The mountain never moves; the students are the avalanche.
- Which exist now, and where: DM-controlled. Establish in play.

### Wandering Brothers & Bodyguard-Ascetics — uncommon but essential

The monastery's interface with the world.

- Monastery teachers on circuit, courier-monks trusted precisely because they own nothing worth bribing, ascetic bodyguards hired by merchants who want protection that doesn't drink.
- Their work: safe passage for people and messages, arbitration nobody can accuse of being bought, quiet teaching in towns that will never see the mountain.
- Trusted the way priests are trusted, for the opposite reason — a priest answers to a hierarchy, a monk visibly answers to nothing you can threaten.

### Novices & Street Ascetics — common enough to be called beggars

- Novices who left or were asked to leave, street ascetics, beggar-mystics, and frauds in borrowed robes performing serenity for coin.
- Regarded as somewhere between holy and homeless, with the distinction left to the observer. Most claims to a mountain lineage are false; the difficulty is that the true ones look identical.
- The tier the monasteries draw from — recruiters watch the begging bowls for the one kid whose stillness isn't performed.
