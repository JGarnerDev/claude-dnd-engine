# Barbarian Graduations

**Projection: legend.** Barbarian cultures build no registries — they build sagas. Their institutions are warbands, bloodlines, and stories, and what those project is the promise that somewhere out there is a person no army wants to meet.

### Saga-Ends & Godwrestlers — extremely rare

The ones the sagas are *about*.

- Warlords whose names end migrations, khans who stitched a hundred feuding clans into one horde, the champion who is claimed — in every telling — to have wrestled something divine and made it yield.
- They shape ages the way arch mages do, but through momentum: where they walk, peoples move. Their deaths are calendar events; their burial sites become borders.
- Which exist now, and where: DM-controlled. Establish in play.

### Warband Champions — uncommon but essential

The reason the village is still there.

- Clan champions, caravan shockguards, mercenary breakers, the professional front line of any culture that fights close. Hired the way wizards are hired, for the same reason: they end problems other people can't.
- Their work is unglamorous protection — holding a ford, walking a herd through raider country, standing in a shield wall so the levies behind them don't run.
- Every saga-end was one of these first. The middle tier is where legends are still just reputations.

### Brawlers & Pit Fighters — common enough to be called savages

- Tavern toughs, pit fighters, angry strong-backs with a local reputation and no discipline to bank it.
- Regarded as somewhere between entertainment and hazard. The name of the class is used as an insult about them, by people who have never met the upper tiers.
- The tier that feeds the middle when a warband recruiter passes through the pit on a good night.
