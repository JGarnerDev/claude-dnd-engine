# Bard Graduations

**Projection: memory.** Bardic institutions — colleges, circuits, courts — project control over what is *remembered*. Wizards decide battles; bards decide which side was right, fifty years later. Every other class's top tier lives in fear of this one's verdict.

### Legend-Makers — extremely rare

The ones who decide what history says.

- Masters of the great colleges, court-voices whose satire has toppled a dynasty without a blade drawn, composers whose one song is the only surviving account of an entire war.
- They shape ages by editing them. A king can win every battle and still lose the century if a legend-maker takes the other side. Most people can name their *works* and not their faces — which is how they like it.
- Which exist now, and where: DM-controlled. Establish in play.

### Circuit Bards & Chroniclers — uncommon but essential

The world's nervous system.

- Court bards, college-trained chroniclers, heralds, negotiators, and the respectable half of every spy network. In a world where long-distance communication runs on couriers (`data/lore/magic.md`), the traveling bard *is* the news.
- Their work: carrying word between cities, keeping genealogies and treaties straight, making a lord look legitimate, making a rival look small. Paid in patronage, protected by custom — harming a herald-bard is one of the few crimes every kingdom agrees on.
- Half of them report to someone. Which half is the interesting question.

### Buskers & Gossip-Mongers — common enough to be called minstrels

- Tavern singers, street performers, rumor-peddlers, wedding musicians of wildly uneven talent.
- Regarded as somewhere between welcome noise and light-fingered nuisance. "Minstrel" said flatly is an accusation.
- The tier the colleges recruit from — one great performance in the right market square can change a life.
