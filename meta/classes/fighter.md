# Fighter Graduations

**Projection: legitimacy.** Fighting institutions — orders, companies, watches — project the *right* to bear arms: banners, oaths, public muster, ranks everyone can read. A knight and a bandit differ mostly in paperwork, and the paperwork is the institution's whole point.

### Generals & Weapon-Masters — extremely rare

The ones whose styles become schools.

- Kingmaker generals, masters whose named techniques outlive them by centuries, the duelist monarchs schedule around. When one takes a side, recruitment on the other side gets harder — armies believe in them the way villages believe in saints.
- They shape ages the conventional way: they win the wars that historians organize the centuries by. Of all top tiers, this is the one history actually records — the bards see to it.
- Which exist now, and where: DM-controlled. Establish in play.

### Knights, Sergeants & Watch Captains — uncommon but essential

The reason there is an "inside the walls."

- Order knights, company sergeants, city watch captains, drillmasters, castellans. Armies exist because this tier exists — levies are farmers until a sergeant has had them for a season.
- Their work: garrisons held, roads patrolled, recruits drilled, sieges endured. Unrecorded, uncelebrated, indispensable — the martial mirror of the working wizard.
- Their oaths are load-bearing. When this tier stops believing in the banner, the kingdom finds out last.

### Militia & Hired Blades — common enough to be called sword-fodder

- Town militia, caravan guards of uneven quality, tavern bouncers, farm lads with a spear and one season's drilling.
- Regarded as somewhere between civic duty and cheap insurance. Professionals call them sword-fodder; professionals were all sword-fodder once.
- The widest bottom tier of any class — and the one the middle tier actively recruits from, every muster day.
