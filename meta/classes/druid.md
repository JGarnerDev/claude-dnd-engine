# Druid Graduations

**Projection: continuity.** The circles project *oldness* — the claim that they were here before the kingdom and will be here after it. No registries, no prices; their institution is the grove itself, and everyone within a week's ride knows exactly where it is and exactly not to touch it.

Per `data/lore/magic.md`: the circles' summit is as rare as the wizards', far less recorded — and an old grove may quietly outmatch the guilds *in its own region* without ever projecting power beyond it.

### Archdruids — extremely rare

The ones storms are rumored to consult.

- Voices of entire regions, keepers of groves older than the surrounding nations, the figure the king's envoy waits three seasons to see and considers it fast.
- They shape ages by *withholding* — the harvest that fails, the pass that stays snowed in, the forest that swallows a road. Recorded history barely mentions them because recorded history is not where they work.
- Which exist now, and where: DM-controlled. Establish in play.

### Grove Keepers & Circuit Druids — uncommon but essential

The agriculture behind the agriculture.

- Grove wardens, circuit druids walking a loop of farm villages, beast-healers, blight-readers, midwives to herds.
- Their work: harvests that come in, murrains that don't spread, weather read early enough to matter. Farming country with a circuit druid and without one are different worlds — the rural mirror of the temple healer.
- Paid in kind, in custom, and in the certainty that the village will hide them if anyone official comes asking.

### Hedge-Witches of the Green — common enough to be called weed-pullers

- Herb-grannies, half-trained wardens, charm-sellers, the odd soul at the wood's edge with a raven and opinions.
- Regarded as somewhere between useful neighbor and unsettling one. Blamed first when the milk sours; consulted first when the fever won't break.
- Live entirely outside anything organized — some are the circles' unschooled cousins, a few are the real thing waiting to be found.
