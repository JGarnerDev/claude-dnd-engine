# Paladin Graduations

**Projection: exemplarity.** Paladin orders project the *standard* — the person everyone else is measured against. Their institutions run on visible perfection: shining wards, public oaths, conspicuous rescue. This makes them the only class whose top tier can be destroyed by a rumor.

### Living Saints of the Sword — extremely rare

The oath, incarnate.

- Order-founders still walking, oath-incarnations whose presence ends plagues and stops routs, the knight whose name is sworn *by*.
- They shape ages as symbols more than swords — kingdoms rally to them, tyrants pre-emptively fear them. And their fall, when it happens, is a historical event: a saint's broken oath does more damage than a war.
- Which exist now, and where: DM-controlled. Establish in play.

### Order Knights & Judges-Errant — uncommon but essential

Justice, where the courts don't reach.

- Order knights on errantry, wardens of roads and shrines, judges-errant whose verdict is accepted because their oath is better collateral than any court's seal.
- Their work: escorting pilgrims, breaking bandit companies, arbitrating feuds in places a king's writ is a rumor. The frontier's supreme court, one rider at a time.
- Held to their order's standard in public, always — a working paladin lives permanently on duty, and the ones who resent that tend not to stay in the middle tier, in either direction.

### Vow-Takers & Hedge Knights — common enough to be called zealots

- Self-sworn vow-takers, zealous squires, hedge knights of dubious oath and dented kit, penitents who mistook fervor for calling.
- Regarded as somewhere between admirable and exhausting. The genuine article at this tier is common; the *durable* article is not — most self-sworn oaths don't survive their first real test.
- The tier the orders recruit from, carefully — the recruiter's craft is telling conviction from performance, and the cost of a mistake is the middle tier's reputation.
