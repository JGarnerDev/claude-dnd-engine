# Ranger Graduations

**Projection: reach.** Ranger institutions — lodges, warden-lines, pathfinder companies — project the trustworthiness of the *map*. Their claim: the edges of the known world are watched, the passes have names, and something will tell you before the danger arrives. Civilization's borders sit where this class says they do.

### Wardens of the Frontier — extremely rare

The ones the maps quote.

- Wardens of entire frontiers, pathfinders who named the passes everyone now uses, beast-lords whose territories function as sovereign states that no treaty mentions.
- They shape ages by deciding where civilization stops — the warden who opens a route moves trade for a century; the one who closes a frontier ends a kingdom's expansion without a battle. Like archdruids, they barely appear in records; unlike archdruids, the *routes* are their monument.
- Which exist now, and where: DM-controlled. Establish in play.

### Guides, Scouts & Bounty Hunters — uncommon but essential

The reason the caravan arrives.

- Caravan pathfinders, border scouts, royal foresters, bounty hunters, the guide every expedition budget lists right after food.
- Their work: routes walked ahead, weather and raiders read early, the lost found, the fled retrieved. Trade exists because this tier keeps the space *between* cities survivable — the road's mirror of the working wizard.
- Solitary by trade but not unorganized — lodges and warden-lines pass word along frontiers faster than any courier, which is why border lords court them.

### Trappers & Village Hunters — common enough to be called poachers

- Trappers, village hunters, fur-runners, and poachers — the distinction being mostly whose forest it legally is.
- Regarded as somewhere between provider and trespasser, often both in the same season. "Poacher" is the insult; half the middle tier answered to it once.
- The tier the lodges recruit from — the kid who comes back from the deep wood on time, every time, gets noticed.
