# Rogue Graduations

**Projection: invisibility.** Rogue institutions are the wizard guilds inverted — same cartel behavior (turf, codes, prices), but built so none of it can be pointed at. No registries, no posted anything, membership deniable.

### Phantoms & Spymasters — extremely rare

Names that are arguments, not people.

- Royal spymasters, guildmothers of shadow networks spanning kingdoms, thieves no one proves exist. Some names are titles passed between holders — killing "the Magpie" changes nothing.
- They don't steal objects; they steal outcomes — successions, wars, treaties. Age-shapers like arch mages, but *unrecorded by design*. History books credit their work to luck and kings.
- Which exist now, and where: DM-controlled. Establish in play, not in generation.

### Working Rogues — uncommon but essential

The criminal economy's rank-and-file. Also the legal one's.

- Fences, locksmiths, guild burglars, smugglers, debt collectors, information brokers, caravan scouts, tax investigators. Half hold honest titles.
- Cities run on them: goods move past tariffs, secrets reach buyers, locks get tested, contracts get "verified." Merchant houses hire them the way shipyards hire wizards.
- The decisive asset in intrigue that combined ritual casting is in war — a court with no eyes-and-ears facing one with a network isn't a rivalry, it's a harvest.

### Cutpurses & Street Thieves — common enough to be called thieves

- Pickpockets, con artists, lookouts, street kids running dice games. Modest talents, caught often, hanged occasionally.
- Regarded as somewhere between vermin and local color. Respectability is never on offer — but pity sometimes is.
- Lives outside guild sanction; the tier that feeds the middle when talent surfaces.
