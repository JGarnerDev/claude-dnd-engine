# Warlock Graduations

**Projection: the debt.** Warlocks build few institutions because the defining relationship is vertical — each one answers *up*, to a patron, before answering to any peer. What structures exist (cults, cells, occult salons) project secrecy of a particular flavor: not the rogue's operational invisibility, but discretion about *whose* signature is on the contract.

### Voices of the Patron — extremely rare

The ones who renegotiated.

- Cult-founders, pact-scholars who read their own contract closely enough to find leverage, the warlock a patron treats as an envoy rather than an asset.
- They shape ages as brokers between the world and things outside it — the treaty with the thing under the lake, the bargain that ended the siege and mortgaged the valley. Their works are attributed to their patrons; the attribution error is their camouflage.
- Which exist now, and where: DM-controlled. Establish in play. (Note the standing shadow in `data/lore/magic.md`: at least one actor treats magical power as something to be *possessed* — patron material, if the DM wants it.)

### Brokers & Pact-Scholars — uncommon but essential

The occult service economy.

- Occult fixers, pact-scholars, cult middle-management, the practitioner merchants quietly retain for problems no guild will invoice.
- Their work: wards against things wards shouldn't be needed for, negotiations with entities, retrievals from places working wizards decline. They occupy the market the guilds refuse to certify — and the guilds' refusal is the business model.
- Every one of them carries a payment schedule the client never sees. Reliability is high; the *cost structure* is the mystery.

### Candle-Bargainers — common enough to be called dabblers

- Desperate one-boon bargainers, dabblers with a candle and a name they shouldn't know, grief-struck souls who said yes to the first voice that answered.
- Regarded with more pity than contempt — everyone knows a story about one, and the story never ends well. The bottom tier of this class is the only one defined by a *mistake* rather than a modest talent.
- The tier the cults recruit from and the patrons collect from. A candle-bargainer who survives the first debt has, by definition, something worth a second look.
