# Cleric Graduations

**Projection: authority.** Clerical hierarchies project divine sanction — the claim that their power is *borrowed from something that outranks kings*. Where wizard guilds post prices, temples post commandments. (Institutional detail belongs to the faiths — see `data/lore/` religion material where it exists.)

### Prophets & Hierophants — extremely rare

The ones the god answers in full sentences.

- Living saints, voices-of-the-god, high hierophants whose miracles mark eras — the plague that stopped at a raised hand, the river turned at a word.
- They shape ages the way arch mages do, but with a constituency: an arch mage acts alone, a prophet moves a faith. Their canonizations and their heresies are both historical events.
- Which exist now, and where: DM-controlled. Establish in play.

### Temple Priests & Chaplains — uncommon but essential

The world's actual medicine.

- Parish priests, temple healers, army chaplains, monastery infirmarians. In a world where true medicine requires a caster (`data/lore/magic.md`), this tier is the difference between an injury and a funeral.
- Their work: healing, funerals, oaths, harvest blessings, keeping the local dead quiet in the ground. A town with a real priest and a town without one are different economic tiers.
- Respectable in a way no wizard manages — different institution, different fears, different courtesies. And watched by their hierarchy as closely as any guild wizard.

### Deacons & Doom-Criers — common enough to be called god-botherers

- Village deacons, wandering friars, relic peddlers, street-corner doom-criers, the devout with more faith than power.
- Regarded as somewhere between harmless and tiresome — until the relic turns out to be real, which is rarely.
- The tier the temples ordain from, and the tier where most fraud in the divine economy lives.
