# Prose Style

How generated prose should read: read-aloud text, session plans, historian records, player-facing notes,
lore. The goal is prose a skeptical reader trusts, with nothing a pattern put there that the writer could
not defend. Read once per task whenever the task writes or edits prose.

Adapted from the `anti-slop` skill in [elithrar/dotfiles](https://github.com/elithrar/dotfiles)
(MIT), with fiction and fantasy tells added for this table.

## Core rule

Make small phrasing changes that remove filler without flattening voice. Preserve facts, names, numbers,
quoted table lines and NPC voice cores. Flag phrasing only when it adds no meaning or repeats a formula;
keep deliberate fragments, parallelism, humor and useful contrast.

The patterns below are **candidates, not a blacklist**. Run every flag through the validation questions
before cutting it. Over-correcting into flat, beige prose is its own failure.

## The tell catalog

- **Meta throat-clearing** — announcing the point before making it: "It is worth noting that…", "Here is
  the tension." Delete the frame; state the thing.
- **Demonstrative kicker** — a vague "This/That + verdict" fragment after a sentence: "That is the whole
  point." It restates the prior sentence. Cut it or fold it in.
- **Puffery adverbs** — genuinely, truly, actually, simply, deeply, fundamentally. Delete. If the claim
  needs the adverb to be true, the claim is weak.
- **Importance-flagging** — "This matters.", "Make no mistake." Show the consequence instead.
- **Clever metaphor flourish** — "a betrayal wearing the costume of a favor." State it plainly.
- **Grandiose stakes** — "will change Aethryn forever", "nothing will ever be the same". Scope to the
  concrete consequence.
- **Rule-of-three reflex** — every list, every description padded to three. Keep two if two is true.
- **Antithesis tic** — "not X, but Y" paragraph after paragraph. Keep the load-bearing one.
- **Correlative bloat** — "not only… but also", "whether… or". Split or simplify.
- **Hedged confidence** — "In many ways…", "One might argue…". Take the position or cut.
- **Section-closing summary** — a last sentence restating the paragraph. Delete.
- **Overused lexicon** — delve, tapestry, realm, landscape, underscore, testament to, navigate
  (figurative), foster, elevate, myriad, pivotal, crucial, intricate, interplay. Swap for the plain word
  or name the actual thing; keep the original only if the plain word is worse.

## Fiction and fantasy tells

The stock moves of model-written fantasy. Same rule: candidates, not bans.

- **Body-language stock** — a breath they didn't know they were holding, a smile that didn't reach the
  eyes, a jaw tightening, a voice barely above a whisper, shivers down a spine, knuckles whitening. Give
  the specific gesture this character would make, or cut it.
- **Abstract weight** — "the weight of X", "the silence stretched", "something shifted", "the world held
  its breath", "the air thick with tension". Name the physical thing in the room.
- **Echo nouns** — whispers of, echoes of, a dance of, a symphony of, a tapestry of. Name the thing.
- **Adjective stacks** — "ancient, crumbling, moss-covered stones". Pick the one detail that matters.
- **Sensory checklist** — sight, sound and smell in every paragraph, often ozone, copper or petrichor.
  One sharp detail beats three generic ones.
- **Weather as mood** — every scene opens on light, sky or wind matching the emotion. Use it when it
  does work.
- **Ominous button** — ending every scene or entry on a one-line portent ("But the darkness was only
  beginning."; "Little did they know…"). Earn it once; never by default.
- **Default fantasy names** — Elara, Kael, Lyra, Seraphina, Thorne, Vex, Aldric, Elowen, Zephyr and
  kin are model reflexes. Build new names from the culture's own sounds (see
  [[worldbuilding-approach]]); never rename an existing entity for this.
- **Capitalized Significance** — The Ancient Evil, The Forgotten Pact. Capitalize proper names, not mood.

## Structural tells

- Uniform paragraph and sentence length. Vary it; some ideas need one sentence.
- Bullet lists where prose argues better, or prose where the items are truly parallel.
- Bold everywhere, so nothing stands out.
- Em-dash as the default connector. Vary with commas, colons, periods, parentheses.
- Symmetrical hedging and tidy "both sides" wrap-ups when the answer is known.

## What to keep

These are voice, not slop. Deleting them is the over-correction failure.

- Earned fragments: a short beat after a long sentence, used sparingly.
- Deliberate parallelism and a strong closing line, when it is the only one in the piece.
- Snark, jokes and running gags (achievements, rumors, the gorilla guerillas).
- An NPC's established voice, even when it uses a flagged pattern on purpose (see [[voice]]).

## Calibration by audience

Match the register; never neutralize tone toward a generic middle.

- **Read-aloud text** (boxed scene text in session plans) — second person, present tense, short. Two to
  four sentences. One or two sharp sensory details, then stop: end on something the party can act on,
  not on a portent. Never tell the party what they feel or decide.
- **DM-facing plans** — plain and scannable. Lines carry a scene cue (see `/review`). No flourish.
- **Historian records** — neutral, past tense, facts first. No foreshadowing, no adjectives doing the
  work of evidence.
- **Player-facing notes and recap slides** — warmer, a little story flavor, still no spoilers and no
  conclusions drawn for the party.
- **Lore and pitches** — the most room for voice, and the most slop-prone. Heaviest pass here.

## Validation questions

For any flagged phrase, ask in order:

1. Does cutting it lose meaning? If yes, it may be content. Keep or reword.
2. Formula or choice? Would the writer defend it if asked?
3. Does it announce rather than deliver? Meta-commentary is almost always cuttable.
4. Heat or light? Cut heat, keep light.
5. Would a skeptical reader trust the sentence more without it?

If a phrase survives all five, leave it.
