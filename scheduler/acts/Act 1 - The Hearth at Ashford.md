Campaign: [[Redwater]]

The party falls into **[[Ashford]]** in the middle of a raid and spends this act earning a
share of the building they saved. The **Cael–Marlow feud** is the reason the town bleeds, and
it is discoverable in full, but it is **backdrop** — the act never requires the party to pick
a side in a war between two lords. What it requires is that they decide whether this town is
theirs.

## The conflict, and why it stays in the background

**[[House Marlow]]**, out of Duskwater, is bleeding **[[House Cael]]**'s border trade using
land raiders it can disavow. **[[Lord Corren Cael]]** is Ashford's lord, is not a fool, and
cannot come. That is the whole machine, and the party can run the entire act treating the
raiders as independent bandits without being wrong about anything that matters to them.

If they dig, it comes apart in order: the matched boots say someone is paying · a name,
**[[Harrow Bane]]** · an intermediary holding Marlow's coin · and under all of it the old
grievance, "the shield that turned," which **[[Richmond Ellesine]]** can tell them firsthand
because he was there. The traitor-reveal is a reward for curiosity, not a gate.

## The spine

**[[The Golden Lioness]]** is the act. It is half-old and half-new: a characterful core that
is genuinely the safest building in town, and a brewery wing that is built, unopened, and
undefended. **[[Sable Wren]]** is running the whole place alone — her crew has not arrived —
and after the party defends it she offers them a provisional share rather than charity.

The ladder is three ticks, earned by deeds and not purchased: **10% → 25% → 40%**. Gold buys
structure, favours buy people, and quest rewards buy the standout unlocks. The brewhouse is
the keystone, because **[[Aashal-Groon Mellenegon Borak]]** in the aquifer underneath will
bless deep water for brewing in exchange for deeds that serve his water and his memory — and
the feud is fouling that water, which is how the backdrop reaches down and touches the reward
loop without anyone having to care about two lords.

## Shape

Four designed sessions, inside the season's eight slots — which leaves roughly four slots of
room, and is why the full three-tick stake ladder is viable instead of compressed.

- **Session 1 — the raid.** Arrival mid-raid, Nella's leftovers, save Sable and salvage what
  is left. The deed that earns the first tick. Nella escapes clean, on purpose.
- **Session 2 — the pursuit.** The red trail after the stolen casks. Nella escapes a second
  time, on her own skill and a fallback the party can watch her use.
- **Session 3 — the cell and the flush.** Wick stages a bled body to panic the town; the
  party gets a name and a prediction.
- **Session 4 — the convergence night.** Jorn's raid and Wick's public declaration land on the
  same night, on the same stage.

The remaining slots are where the stake ladder's third tick, the brewhouse, and whatever the
party has started chasing get paid off.

Nothing in these four sessions reopens the way home. **[[The Wanderer's Boon]]** stays in
Odibli's hand and stays inert, and one line of Session 1 should say so out loud — otherwise
the season reads as a detour instead of the errand the relic sent them on.

## Difficulty

Act-level, and deliberately not in `meta/difficulty.md` — this curve belongs to this act.

- **Opening — 5/10.** Standard attrition, standard lethality. Session 1's fight is leaderless
  fodder and is meant to be winnable by a party that just fell out of the sky.
- **Late — 8/10.** Standard-to-grinding attrition, dangerous lethality. Lethal is held back
  for a 10/10 the act never reaches.
- **Opt-in escalation throughout.** The party can take harder content for bigger reward; the
  base-building ally call-ins are the pressure valve on the ramp.

Loot carries a large share of the progression weight this act. The rules for it are in
`## Loot` below.

## Loot

This section overrides `meta/rewards.md` for Act 1, as that file allows. Decided 2026-10-04 by
Jeff (questions 1–16 of `questionnaires/redwater/act-1-loot.md`). Questions 17–27, on Session 29

### Levels

- **The whole party reaches level 5 before the December finale**, ideally before the last
  session's big fight. They start at 4.
- **Levels are given one PC at a time**, at a moment the DM judges climactic, rather than as a
  party-wide tick. The DM is trying this out and will adjust it if it plays badly.
- **Tension with `meta/rewards.md`, still unresolved:** that file requires the next level's
  trigger to be announced before it lands, because an unannounced level reads as arbitrary.
  One way to keep both rules: name a PC's coming moment when it is set, and keep only the exact
  timing a surprise. The DM has not ruled on this.

### Items

- **The party is under-equipped. Good, cool items are overdue,** and items are the main way the
  party grows this act.
- **One personalised item per PC** across the act, as a floor.
- **Five Uncommons** (one per PC) **plus one Rare.** The Rare is not assigned yet. Ideas are
  wanted for a Rare per PC *and* a shared party Rare, and the pick comes from those.
- **Handout order and sources are open.** They get decided once the items are chosen, or when
  a story moment suggests one. Where there is no good source, create a moment or an enemy for
  the item instead of letting it drop from nowhere.
- **Every item arrives with a history of at most three sentences**: who made it, who carried
  it, where it was found. The menus in `meta/loot-options/` stay plot-free, and the history is
  attached at the moment it is handed over.
- **Clues back to the feud are decided item by item.** A raider's gear *may* carry a Marlow or
  Duskwater mark, but no item gets one by default.
- **Surprise handouts.** Players never see their menus. The DM picks and the item just turns up.
- **Session 29's Common goes to Dip,** not Iggy. Iggy is the DM's own PC, and Ben is the one who
  pushed on levelling. Iggy's item lands later in the act.

### Gold and consumables

- **What gold is for:** the party decides, and building at the Lioness is actively encouraged
  (250–500 gp a room). Coin never buys stake percentage. Stake is earned by deeds only.
- **Shops:** Ashford sells healing potions at a raised price until the raids stop. No scrolls.
- **Ale:** the Lioness's regular brews are not magical. Magical brews are something
  [[Sable Wren]] deliberately makes and sells, once the brewhouse is running on Asher's water.
  Session 29 can hint at this, but grants nothing.
- **Gear carried out of Barovia works normally** in Aethryn. Only things bound to Barovia's
  curse go quiet: the afflictions, and [[The Wanderer's Boon]].
