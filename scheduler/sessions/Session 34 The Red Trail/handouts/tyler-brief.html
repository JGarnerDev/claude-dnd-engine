Tyler's Way In

What you need to know to build your character. Jeff's sessions end with you in a cell, and Ben's sessions start from there.

The game

The group plays two campaigns in turns, like seasons of a show. One is Curse of Strahd, run by Kellan in the land of Barovia. The other is Aethryn, a world the group built together. Jeff is running Aethryn now, and Ben runs it next. The same party travels between both worlds.

The party is level 5, so your character starts at level 5 too.

Where you are

Ashford is a small river town on a border, with too few guards and a lord who answers its letters but sends no soldiers. Its new tavern, the Golden Lioness, is run by Sable Wren. Its sheriff, Mara Tull, holds the town together with a handful of deputies.

For months the town has been raided by a band that fights like a business: matched boots, planned crews, no wasted violence. When people say the leader's name, Harrow Bane, the room goes quiet.

The party arrived in the middle of one of those raids, saved the tavern, and rode out after the ale the raiders stole. Overhead, Aethryn has two suns and a broken moon that still sheds pieces of itself.

Your entrance

While the party is away, a raider from Bane's band walks into Ashford, lays his weapons on the sheriff's step, and surrenders. He sits in Mara's cell and says nothing. Half the town wants him hanged by the end of the week. When the party gets back, he's the first thing they hear about.

That raider is your character.

Where it goes

Ben's sessions are a holiday pilgrimage to the far north, where the veil between worlds is thin. It starts in Cinto, a port town full of pilgrims, just before the festival season. Your character is how the party gets there, so talk with Ben about what links you to that trip.

The Gods' Door

Anyone raised in this part of the world has heard of Deorum Ostium, the Gods' Door, on the frozen northern coast. Long ago the gods withdrew from the world and stopped speaking to it directly, and the Door is said to be the last place they touched before they left.

It's a building of ice that is never finished. Pilgrims of every faith go there, and the one rule is that each of them carves something into it: a picture, a prayer, a whole book, or one line added to someone else's words. Monks cut new chambers all the time. Some go so deep that nobody has seen their carvings in centuries, and people say there are incantations hidden down there, waiting for someone to read them aloud.

It's the most important pilgrimage in the known world. The old way goes overland through the north. Ships also make the crossing over the cold northern sea.

Set, and yours to decide

Already set

  • You rode with Bane's band.
  • You walked in and surrendered on your own.
  • You haven't said a word since.
  • You're level 5.

Yours

  • Your name, race and class.
  • Why you quit the band.
  • Why you've stayed silent.
  • What you want from the party.

What your character knows about the band from the inside, and how it ties into the trip north, is worth working out with Jeff and Ben together.
