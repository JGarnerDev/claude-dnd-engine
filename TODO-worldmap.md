# World Map Todo

## Decisions Made

- **Scale:** Full world map
- **Wind direction:** West to East (prevailing westerlies)
  - West-facing coasts and west sides of mountain ranges = wet, lush, forested
  - East sides of mountain ranges = dry (rain shadow) — deserts and scrubland go here
  - East-facing coasts = drier, especially if cut off by inland mountains
- **Coastline:** Done
- **Mountains:** Done — ranges established with chain direction
- **Rivers:** Done — flowing east/southeast off ranges, reach coast
- **Lakes:** Done — placed at mountain bases and along river courses

## Current Status: Step 4b — Hills

Hills go **before biomes** — they frame the terrain shape that biome textures get painted on top of.

- [ ] Add a band of hills at the base of every mountain range — mountains taper, they don't just stop
- [ ] Hills follow the same orientation as the range they belong to — don't scatter them randomly
- [ ] Steeper ranges (like the dramatic central peaks) have a narrow hill band; gentler ranges have a wider one
- [ ] Old eroded ranges that have worn down over time become rolling hills — use this if you want ancient-feeling terrain
- [ ] Roads and passes cut through hills, not over them — keep that in mind for later

---

## Step 5 — Deserts and Dry Zones

Given westerly winds, dry zones go on the **east side of mountain ranges** and in **deep interiors** far from any coast.

- [ ] Mark rain shadow zones east of each major range
- [ ] The further east a flat region sits from the coast, the drier it gets
- [ ] Deserts are mostly rocky and cracked — sandy dunes are only one small part of most deserts
- [ ] A dry zone doesn't have to be a full desert — scrubland and dry grassland count too

---

## Step 6 — Plains and Lowlands

- [ ] Flat land near rivers = fertile farmland (good for kingdoms and trade routes)
- [ ] Flat land far from water = grassland or scrubland
- [ ] Low coastal areas near river mouths = marshes and wetlands
- [ ] Big river deltas are flat, wet, and very fertile — often heavily populated

---

## Step 7 — Forests and Swamps

Given westerly winds:
- [ ] Dense forest goes on **west-facing coasts** and **west sides of mountains**
- [ ] Swamps and bogs go in **low flat areas** where water pools with nowhere to drain
- [ ] Jungle goes near the equator (hot + wet year-round)
- [ ] Tundra and sparse trees go far north or south, or at very high elevation
- [ ] The dry east side of ranges gets sparse woodland, scrub, or grassland — not dense forest

---

## Step 8 — Special Features (pick what fits)

- [ ] Volcanoes — one or two, near mountain range endpoints or isolated in the ocean
- [ ] Caldera lake — a volcano that collapsed and filled with water (dramatic, good story location)
- [ ] Cliffs — dramatic coastline where hard rock meets the sea directly
- [ ] Gorges and canyons — where rivers cut through rock over a long time (good dungeon territory)
- [ ] Hot springs — near volcanic areas
- [ ] Cave systems — under flat pale-rock (limestone) regions
- [ ] Glaciers — on the highest peaks, or far north/south

## Step 9 — Coastline Detail Pass

Do this last — it's cosmetic but makes the map feel real.

- [ ] Sandy beaches in calm bays between headlands
- [ ] Rocky cliffs and sea stacks where waves pound hard rock directly
- [ ] Wide muddy estuaries where big rivers meet the sea
- [ ] Fjords on glaciated coastlines (long narrow inlets, very steep walls)
- [ ] Barrier islands or sandbars off flat river-delta coasts

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Rivers that never reach the coast or a lake
- Lakes with no outlet river (unless in a desert)
- Forests on the dry east side of mountains
- Deserts on the wet west side of mountains
- A coast that's perfectly smooth with no bays, headlands, or inlets
- Mountain clusters with no clear ridge direction (look like bumpy hills, not ranges)
